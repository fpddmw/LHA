# Connected visual changes and local repair

Status: human-assisted reasoning hypothesis. A connected-field strategy has not been validated against local wording in a controlled generation test.

"Small" describes the requested extent of an edit. Its spatial reliability depends on the renderer's actual conditioning and controls. A video-plus-text request supplies no guaranteed pixel lock. At the same time, the present evidence does not justify a universal claim that these models cannot perform local edits.

Many apparently local properties are carried by several visible relationships. A glowing tabletop rim involves the contour, material, brightness gradient and nearby illumination; light on the legs can satisfy the color word while missing the intended part. A robe affects the silhouette, the chair's visible support and overlap with the book. Page light affects surface texture and the visibility of the open leaves.

In [[case-bernini-003-before-v3|the fantasy study]], preserving a fitted garment and placing restrained light along page margins describes a connected, legible scene. Both replay arms proposed related revisions. This supports identifying a concrete design conflict; their repair effectiveness remains unmeasured.

One strategy worth testing is to express a coherent target state for the interacting region: how its surfaces, contours and lighting fit together over the visible action. The region is a reasoning focus, not a mask. The task may also favor returning to an earlier input, keeping a satisfactory candidate, or attempting a genuinely local change when the renderer supports one.

The useful knowledge is which relationships carry the effect and what information the input supplies. A blanket instruction to regenerate everything would discard that distinction.
