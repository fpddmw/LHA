# Scene identity and stylization

Status: human-assisted interpretation of development cases. The severity boundary remains task-dependent and unvalidated.

Geometry describes shape and spatial organization. Stylization describes how a scene is represented; it can itself change geometry. A useful judgment concerns the consequence of a change for the current request, rather than assigning every changed contour to damage.

Consider what carries identity in this scene: an explicitly named landmark, a person's recognizable appearance, a hand using a tool, the support beneath a seated person, or an arrangement that makes the event legible. A coarse contour or a simplified texture may carry those relationships successfully. Replacing a distinctive landmark or disconnecting a hand from its tool can change the event or identity. Which distinction matters comes from the request and scene, not a universal pixel-fidelity rule.

In [[case-bernini-027-after-v1|the etching case]], both replay arms elevated architectural silhouette preservation to a core requirement and proposed another source generation. Historical assessments disagree about the severity. For a broad etching request, that disagreement remains open. An explicit request to reproduce the architecture accurately would give the silhouette a different role; this is a contrastive hypothetical, not another measured case.

In [[case-bernini-003-before-v3|the fantasy study]], a robe obscures chair support. Visibility loss and object deletion are competing explanations. The observed occlusion is available evidence; hidden geometry is unknown. Inspecting [[wiki-connected-change|connected clothing and support relationships]] may suggest a more informative design revision.

Use these distinctions to interpret a consequence and its uncertainty. They do not prescribe stopping. The user's priorities and the quality of available alternatives determine the decision. Further evidence would compare task-matched judgments and generated alternatives while keeping the evaluation definition fixed.
