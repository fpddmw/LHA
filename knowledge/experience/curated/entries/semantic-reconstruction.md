# EX-semantic-reconstruction: Remove preservation commitments that defeat the target

Revision 1. Approved for use under the goal-directed revision authorized on 2026-11-02. Working judgment from F001 goal review and the user's clarified adaptation intent.

**When the requested world changes what the characters are doing, reconstruct the event's purpose and causal relationships. Use source identities, movement and screen relationships where they support that event. Remove preservation commitments that keep the output functioning as the old scene.**

## A precise prompt can solve the wrong problem

The original F001 request asked for a cinematic Wuxia scene, wide-sleeved Hanfu and a mountain training platform. Its old Planner added continuous basketball choreography as a core requirement. A later Planner kept the ball, rebounds, shot and hoop while relocating the new world to clothing, buildings and light. Changing a basketball support into timber leaves its function as a scoring target intact. More exact compliance with that prompt could still leave the intended adaptation incomplete.

This is a transferable design problem: a schoolroom rebuilt as a ship's bridge can still read as a lesson if everyone continues teaching at a blackboard; changing that function is a creative decision, not a materials correction. In a request to illustrate an unchanged classroom lesson, that same activity should remain. These examples distinguish task scopes; they are not rendered case evidence or compulsory mappings.

## Borrow performance material at the useful level

A lifted arm, approach, recoil or shared gaze can support more than one event. Develop a purpose, recipient and consequence that fit the requested world and the visible source. Keep the correspondence that makes this a meaningful adaptation; choose the remaining action, props and staging as a connected proposal. A genre label alone supplies neither a unique story nor permission for unrelated changes.

An object's function often holds the old event in place. Decide whether the target needs that function before protecting the object's appearance or motion. Sometimes the same function should survive in a new form; sometimes its removal or replacement is central to the transformation. Removing an old prop is only part of the job: the resulting characters still need a readable relationship and progression.

## Spend precision on the target's dependencies

Detailed preservation can burden a scene with contacts, trajectories and exclusions that serve an abandoned activity. Remove those commitments first. Then make the target's decisive relationships concrete using the style library and the actual shot. Favor a coherent, achievable performance over a crowded list of fine-grained interactions. Preserve ambition in the event and visual world; simplify dependencies that contribute little to either.

Natural sleeves, shadow and occlusion can participate in that design. Judge the visible preparation, exchange and response rather than assuming concealed detail succeeded. A quieter or cleaner image earns its place through the target it expresses, not through its ability to escape inspection.
