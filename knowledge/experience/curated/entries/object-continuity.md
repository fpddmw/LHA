# EX-object-continuity: Keep the target interaction coherent through transitions

Revision 4. Approved for use; goal-directed semantic revision authorized on 2026-11-02.

**Bind continuity to the object or interaction the target scene needs. For a retained action, concise source-linked roles can preserve useful motion. For an adapted action, establish the new participant's role and progression rather than carrying over the old prop and trajectory automatically.**

## Establish what participates before preserving its path

A phone retrieval can depend on the same object remaining in the hand through occlusion. A narrative adaptation can replace that phone's function or remove it from the event. Preserve a source trajectory when it helps the intended interaction; otherwise let the new action determine contact, movement and release. A replaced object needs coherent involvement in its new role, even when its shape or path changes.

F001 showed an extra ball while another was airborne; later detailed choreography still produced broken possession and an extra player. F006 v1 showed a second phone-like object during retrieval. These observations identify inconsistent instance binding. They do not make the source ball or phone obligatory in every adaptation. In F001, the earlier decision to require basketball itself now needs revision; see the [case interpretation](../evidence/semantic-goal-review-20261102.md).

## Describe the useful binding

For an action the user wants retained, make its existing participant and contact relationship clear. Add transition detail when it resolves a consequential ambiguity. F006 v2 described concealment, retrieval and raising, and the saved review observed no duplicate; exact hand/phone motion remained uncertain. This favors selective sequence information over either a ban on timing or a lengthy choreography by default.

For a new event, express the dependency that makes it legible: an offered object reaches a recipient, a gesture produces a visible response, or a tool remains connected to what it acts upon. These are design relationships, not predetermined scripts. Prefer a readable realization with the fewest unnecessary precise bindings while keeping the target action substantial.

## Inspect continuity in the resulting event

Inspect retrieval, occlusion, contact and release where they matter to the chosen event. An authorized replacement differs from an object unexpectedly multiplying or disappearing mid-interaction. Natural occlusion can suit the scene; look at the visible approach and consequence to assess the exchange, and retain uncertainty about concealed details.

When an incoherent duplicate is already in an input, compare an earlier usable candidate or source for the needed information. When the candidate has successfully replaced an obsolete source prop, develop that gain. Reintroducing the old object to repair a historical preservation complaint can undo the adaptation.
