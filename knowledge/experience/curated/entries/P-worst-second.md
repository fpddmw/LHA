# EX-P-worst-second: Judge the work by its worst second and its faces, hands and held props

**Before you accept a candidate, open the places where renderers fail: faces, hands, held props and contact points; the first and last second; and the planes you did not describe in the prompt. A viewer remembers the worst moment, and an overview or an attractive opening does not show it.**

## Where the defects were

Across the preview's train tasks the defects the judge scored down were seldom visible in the opening frames:

- **Contact points.** The woman's fingers softened and fused where they met the keyboard, with the arm behind the child smoothed into the shoulder. In another candidate the laptop's keys lost definition and melted into the bed while hair strands merged into the headboard's pattern and the faces looked smoothed ([v5 cases](../evidence/v5-train-cases.json), sample 001).
- **Held props.** A carried beam bowed, changed its end shape from square to rounded, or warped in a short interval while the minifigure's face print alternated between smile designs and its hairpiece vanished and returned (sample 026). A glove surrogate changed silhouette between frames and blended into a white barrier.
- **The first half-second.** In one converted scene a smooth photographic tailgate panel carrying the source's shadows filled the frame for about 0.6 s before the brick surface appeared.
- **The last second.** In a jungle-ruin candidate the final frames showed a shifted wall and dark foreground while the first three-quarters looked polished.

Online reviewers did not always catch these. The Critic's report on the melted-keyboard candidate named a misplaced sconce and said the interaction was plausible, and the Planner delivered that candidate after one generation. A defect not in the report is not necessarily absent.

## How to apply it

1. Ask the reviewer for, or perform, a close look at the named focal parts and the final second. Use native consecutive frames or a crop. See [OBSERVE_EVENTS](../../../creative-loop/OBSERVE_EVENTS.md).
2. Compare the first frame and the last frame with the middle against the planes you described.
3. If the report does not mention hands, held objects or the end of the clip, treat that as a gap and check them yourself.
4. A stable-looking image with little motion can hide an incomplete event. Check the target action, not only the pretty frames.
5. Natural occlusion leaves details unobserved; do not count them as correct.

**Evidence.** Frames and v5 reasons for train samples 001, 005 and 026; F001, F005 and F006 for earlier transition defects. The sampled frames confirmed the brick and keyboard observations; the end-of-clip finding is from a judge's reasoning and a sampled last frame.
