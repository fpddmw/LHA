# Renderer entrances

Current recommendations below come from the same entry summaries as the question map. Read the complete account for renderer-specific evidence and counterexamples. [Historical renderer profiles](evidence/renderer-profile-20261102.md) retain the recorded setups and earlier interpretations; current entry revisions own current advice.

## Bernini

- [EX-P-compare-retained](entries/P-compare-retained.md): Compare every retained candidate on how fully the world was rebuilt, whether the moment is still recognizable, and how stable and clean it is. The newest or the most repaired version is not automatically the best, and a polished result that kept the old scene is not finished.
- [EX-P-counterparts](entries/P-counterparts.md): Map each important source element to a specific, fitting counterpart (a glove to a C-shaped brick hand, a plank to a stack of tan plates) and name the carrier of each effect. Generic theme decoration does not do this work.
- [EX-P-every-plane](entries/P-every-plane.md): List each visible plane (subject, props, ground, background, sky, light) and say how the target medium builds it. A rebuilt figure inside an untouched photographic scene reads as a collage, and a few theme props leave the old place intact.
- [EX-P-identity](entries/P-identity.md): Preserve the person at role level and keep salient attributes (age group, skin tone, build) unless the new world changes them. Keep identity wording short and spend design effort around the face; long facial inventories rebuild a different person.
- [EX-P-medium-logic](entries/P-medium-logic.md): Write the construction rules of the target medium (what makes a mark, a surface, a shadow) and what the light does to each plane. Style names and adjectives alone leave the renderer to guess, and lamps alone do not change the lighting.
- [EX-P-moment](entries/P-moment.md): Before the first generation, write down who acts, what happens, in what order, where, and from which camera position. Keep that skeleton recognizable and rebuild everything else in the target world.
- [EX-P-spend-attempts](entries/P-spend-attempts.md): Use remaining generations to try a genuinely different construction, input or scope, not a restated version of what failed. Keep earlier candidates as options. In a fixed-budget task, use the full allowance before choosing.
- [EX-P-worst-second](entries/P-worst-second.md): Look closely at focal faces, hands, held props and contacts, the first and last second, and the planes you never described. A strong opening or a clean overview can hide melted keys, fused fingers, a source-like first half-second or a collapsing end.
- [EX-B-chain-drift](entries/B-chain-drift.md): On Bernini, five train-sample continuations from an earlier candidate moved stability by -1.0 to +1.1 with no consistent direction. Continue from a candidate only when it holds what you need next; otherwise restart from the source with a rewritten full prompt.
- [EX-B-emission-binding](entries/B-emission-binding.md): On Bernini, requested emission appeared under a table or on arches instead of on the intended perimeter. Describe the glowing edge and the unlit support as a contrast, and look at where the light lands.
- [EX-B-held-props](entries/B-held-props.md): Under heavy conversion Bernini softens fingers into keyboards, bends or reshapes carried objects, flips face prints between frames, and lets a prop detach from the hand. Describe the grip and the prop's rigid shape, and inspect them closely.
- [EX-B-identity-rebuild](entries/B-identity-rebuild.md): Detailed hair, age and glasses descriptions reconstructed different people on Bernini across several tasks, and heavy scene-wide rewrites did too. Keep identity wording short, favor a source that still shows the person, and check every person after each pass.
- [EX-B-partial-default](entries/B-partial-default.md): When the prompt concentrates on a subject, Bernini converts it and keeps photographic scenery, including with a loop-written prompt that tells it to preserve the source. List the planes to rebuild and keep preservation to one clause.
- [EX-B-text-budget](entries/B-text-budget.md): Bernini accepts 512 encoder tokens including its official prefix. Long preservation clauses use space that construction clauses need, and prompts over the limit are refused before any render.
- [EX-C-craft](entries/C-craft.md): In a brick, paper, felt or cut-paper world the medium is made of pieces. A subject built from the material in a photographic scene reads as a collage; name how each plane is assembled from the material.
- [EX-C-film-world](entries/C-film-world.md): In recasts into a film's production design, the environment converted readily. The failures in the train evidence were on the people: replaced, left in source look as cut-outs, or mismatched in light.
- [EX-C-hand-drawn](entries/C-hand-drawn.md): The train split holds anime, painting and comic requests but this library has no scored runs for them. Apply the shared principles and treat any style-specific guidance as a hypothesis to test.
- [EX-C-long](entries/C-long.md): A long request names planes, costume, light and a camera behavior. Carry them in order of what makes the world recognizable, and check each named item in the delivered video, because a long list can exceed what one prompt carries.
- [EX-C-short](entries/C-short.md): A short request gives the target world and little else. Two preview short tasks lost the source's layout and camera position when the prompt followed the request's level of detail; write the source's arrangement and its counterparts into the prompt.
