# EX-design-conflict: An authored design can erase the evidence of an activity

Version: 1. Status: draft; historical online observations plus a mechanism interpretation. Scope: the recorded Bernini study task; broader transfer is a hypothesis. Reworks R7 and the realization component of I3.

**A preservation failure can originate in the visual proposal itself.** A costume, light effect or prop may be visually successful while covering the relationships that make the original activity intelligible. Repeating the preservation instruction leaves that conflict in place.

## Understand the conflict

The magical-study request left the costume open. Planner added a long robe. The second prompt explicitly requested a clearly visible slatted chair and the original furniture arrangement, yet its review described the robe concealing the chair back, seat and legs. The issue was not simply whether a chair existed. The reader's support and posture could no longer be read from the visible person-chair-desk relationship.

The third prompt retained the fitted jacket and trousers, changed their color and embroidery, and explicitly described chair structure. Its review reported the seated relationship readable again. The fantasy direction remained. This suggests examining whether a chosen realization competes with the information that gives the activity its meaning.

## What this can change in a proposal

When a loss follows an optional authored addition, reconsider what that addition contributes and which relations it obscures. A different costume realization, glow placement or prop arrangement may express the same visual direction with different occlusion and contrast. The unit of judgment is the relationship that must remain readable, not a list of original objects.

If the user explicitly requested the conflicting feature, that feature remains part of the task. Find a compatible realization or explain the remaining tradeoff. This case does not authorize dropping user requirements, prescribing fitted clothing, or avoiding ambitious design.
