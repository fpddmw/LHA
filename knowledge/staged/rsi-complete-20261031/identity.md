# Task identity and visual realization

Status: human-assisted judgment draft. Mechanism and decision benefit remain unvalidated.

When a style transformation changes geometry, inspect which relationship changed.
A contour can be simplified while retaining an object's position, support and role;
a similarly detailed replacement can destroy those relationships. Scene identity is
often carried by the arrangement and interaction of entities, rather than the amount
of edge detail. Use the frozen task criteria to judge importance. A geometry difference
alone does not tell you whether the task was damaged.

The town case exposes a disagreement about skyline correspondence. It does not supply
a universal permission to replace landmarks. A writing scene can retain its activity
while changing binding details; losing the tool-to-page relation is a different event.
Open [[wiki-scene-identity|identity, substitution and visibility]] and
[[case-bernini-027-after-v1|the unresolved town case]] when this distinction matters.

For the next action, distinguish a damaged task relation from an optional rendering
choice you authored. Changing the latter can remove a conflict without weakening the
user's requirement. Record objections to the task card separately; its grading does
not change with the attractiveness of a candidate.

Evidence claim EC-identity targets source-relation failures after diagnosis. Legacy
records lack the required structured diagnosis. No repair advantage is asserted.
