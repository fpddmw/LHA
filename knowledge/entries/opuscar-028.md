# 028 | Marty: 1955 Kinescope on a Living-Room TV

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A viewer enters a television program from a living room; screen artifacts and an invitation to dance organize the nested experience.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

A warm exterior living room and cool monochrome television image establish two visual languages. Curved glass, scan lines, ghosting, and screen light connect them. Scan lines remain after the camera passes through the glass, maintaining a visible media layer.

The visible boundary determines where each treatment belongs: scan lines and phosphor softness inhabit the program, while warm illustration and reflected screen light describe the room. This offers a way to develop a new mixed-media proposal with explicit regional roles. A whole-frame recording treatment would be a different choice from introducing a television as a new object.

Within the program, hesitation beside a column leads to an approach, an offered hand, and a responding smile. This complete proposal embeds a scene in another viewing environment. Its scope differs from a local request for a recorded-video appearance.

### Original author's design explanation

A 1955 kinescope playing on a wood-cabinet TV in a warm Bronx living room: scanlines, phosphor glow, a rounded bulging screen, ghosting and snow, all black-and-white except Clawd. Marty began as a live 1953 TV play, so the film is shown as television.

### Complete original style prompt

A 1950s kinescope playing on a wood-cabinet television. Inside the screen: black-and-white with a cool phosphor tint (#DCE6EA whites), soft (half resolution, scaled up), halation, ghosting, tearing bands, a rolling hum bar, light snow, lifted blacks, flicker, grain; only the story's hero keeps its colour (e.g. #D97757), under the same artefacts. Tube: rounded corners, sides bowing outward, about 330 scanlines, glass reflections. Outside, a warm flat-illustrated living room (ochre wallpaper, wood, a table lamp), lit warm by the lamp; the screen casts a clear cold-blue glow on nearby walls. Signature shot: hold about 2 s with the TV filling 60% of the frame, then push through the glass (4-5 s) until the broadcast fills the frame, scanlines visible, then steady TV-studio mid shots. Reference: director Delbert Mann's live Philco Television Playhouse dramas. Avoid: heavy snow, a dull, evenly lit room.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. tv | 0.8 seconds

Ma's parlour, lamp and rotary phone at left, her wing chair at right: the Stardust Ballroom on the TV, and the camera pushes in through the glass.

#### 2. two | 0.9 seconds

Two-shot across the dance floor, scanlines still on: Clawd, in a butcher's paper cap and striped tie, walks over from the pillar toward Clara; the camera tracks right.

#### 3. ask | 0.8 seconds

Medium on the ask: Clawd holds out its hand, and Clara lifts her eyes to it and smiles; slight push with a drift right.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Marty: a white butcher's paper cap and a striped tie, shy by the pillar. The push through the glass takes us from the living room into the broadcast; it works up the nerve and crosses the floor in the two-shot, and the last cut lands on the ask, hand out, with Clara's smile arriving just after.
