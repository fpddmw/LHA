# 017 | Going My Way: Backlit Gothic Stained Glass

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A stained-glass window contains a performance and projects color into its surroundings, connecting image space with the viewing environment.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Lead divisions, streaked glass, grisaille faces, and backlit glow establish the material logic. Figures perform within the window; the final view of colored light on the church floor connects the glass image to exterior space.

Borrowing this medium involves deciding which shapes are glass, where the lead divisions follow them, and where transmitted color falls outside the window. Those relationships distinguish a luminous material from a flat colored partition. A new proposal can choose its own subject and palette while explaining the glass's placement and lighting.

Star emblems, the choir, and conducting gestures come from the subject. A broader comparison concerns how a medium can represent a story while affecting its surroundings, and how its divisions shape figures.

### Ingredients

- Leaded pot-metal glass with grisaille
- Chartres blue quarries
- Harry Clarke jewel colour

### Original author's design explanation

Backlit Gothic stained glass: leaded pot-metal pieces with grisaille-painted faces, Chartres blue, ruby borders, and coloured light pooling on the church floor. Father O'Malley's boys' choir is set into the window under a gold star for 'Swinging on a Star'.

### Complete original style prompt

A backlit Gothic stained-glass window filling 80-90% of frame height and ~60% of width, neighbours glowing at edges. Every shape is cut glass: lightness varied ±8% per piece, streaks, seed bubbles, a brown edge mat, black lead (about 1/250 of frame width). Sky and empty ground: diamond quarries (about 1/30 of frame width); the border is ruby with beads. The main figure is at least half the window's height, in dark-brown grisaille line. Palette by role: background cobalt #1B3F9E, border ruby #B3121F, highlights gold #E3A526, lead #16161A; skin pale amber; scenery deep greens. Only the hero the story centres on is orange glass (e.g. #D97757). Bright glass blooms into the leads; coloured light spills onto floor and sill. Camera: tilt down, pan, settle on the pooled light. References: Chartres Cathedral; Harry Clarke. Avoid: flat fills, random cuts, dark empty wall.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. star | 1.3 seconds

Starts on the gold star medallion and the SWINGING ON A STAR banderole, then tilts down the lancet onto Clawd as Father O'Malley, conducting the glass boys' choir.

#### 2. choir | 0.6 seconds

Medium on the glass choir: boys in red cassocks and surplices singing, the camera panning right past Clawd's baton arm.

#### 3. nave | 0.6 seconds

Cut down into the nave: the window's colours pooled on the marble aisle between the pews, altar candles lit; the camera tilts down toward the floor.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Father O'Malley inside the glass, its body cut from the warmest terracotta glass, with a straw boater, Roman collar, baton and songbook. It conducts on the beat while the boys' mouths open and close out of step. One tilt carries us from the star to Clawd, then hard cuts go to the choir and down to the coloured light on the floor. Runs 1.8× fast.
