# 065 | Unforgiven: Remington Western Oil · Sunset & Nocturne

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

One oil-painting method accommodates sunset and rainy night, allowing time to change the relationship between light and the figure.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Long horizontal sky strokes and form-following marks sit over clear large shapes. They support both gold-red silhouettes and a blue-green night with warm windows. The two palettes correspond to different times rather than a single all-day scheme.

A raised head at a grave and a rainy ride into town are joined by a hard cut. The figure continues while setting and mood change. The case compares a persistent medium with locally changing expression.

### Original author's design explanation

Thick Western oil painting in two keys: Remington's red-gold plains at sunset and his blue-green nocturnes lit by warm windows. Unforgiven opens and closes on a silhouette at a grave against the sunset, and its showdown comes in a town on a rainy night.

### Complete original style prompt

Frederic Remington-style Western oil painting. Primary register, for anything before full dark: sunset, a red-gold sky (orange into deep red) in long horizontal strokes, the sun on the horizon, land and figures dark warm silhouettes, thin warm rim sunward. Second register, only after dark: a blue-green nocturne (sky #17333A, moonlit edges #9CC4BC, warm window light #FFCC6A on dark ground #1E1C16, rain). Broad, readable shapes first, then loaded strokes about 1/80 of frame width wide, 3–6 times as long, soft broken ends, each within ±6% brightness of the colour beneath, following the surface. Light canvas texture. Low horizon, sky 50–60% of the frame, figures small to medium. Camera: one slow push from a silhouetted wide to the figure. References: Remington's late nocturnes; cinematographer Jack N. Green (*Unforgiven*, 1992). Avoid: smooth gradients; hard-edged flat bands; shattered shapes; grey figures in warm scenes; wide pans.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. grave | 1.4 seconds

One push from the whole sunset (farmhouse, windmill, lone tree, the cross in front of the sun) in to Clawd as Munny at the grave; it lifts its head and brings the rifle up across its body.

#### 2. town | 1.1 seconds

Hard cut to Big Whiskey on a rainy night: Clawd rides a dun horse down the muddy street past the lit windows of Greeley's saloon, the camera pushing toward its dripping hat brim.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays William Munny: a wide black hat, a long coat collar, a rifle. The opening silhouette and the grave are one move, the head lifting just as the push arrives; the only cut jumps to rain and night as Clawd rides into town. 1:1 time, no slow motion.
