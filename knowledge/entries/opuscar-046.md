# 046 | The Sting: Saturday Evening Post Illustration

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A magazine-illustration betting room narrows through one approach to a secret signal, bringing the viewer into the exchange when the figure looks at the camera.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Planes of light, form-following strokes, and varied contours model people and a fully described period setting. The source connects the film's chapter cards with magazine-cover traditions, using that illustration context to reorganize the scene.

The single move advances from the room to a finger touching the nose, then a lowered hat brim and a wink. The action implicates the viewer in the con. A character's relationship with the camera becomes part of the design.

### Original author's design explanation

Warm, clean magazine illustration in the Leyendecker–Rockwell line: blocky lit planes, parallel strokes, crisp dark contours. The Sting's own chapter cards imitate Saturday Evening Post covers, so its 1936 betting parlour is drawn the same way.

### Complete original style prompt

1930s Saturday Evening Post cover illustration. Realist figures, correct proportions, expressive faces, built from blocky planes (lit, shadow, warm bounce from below) filled with blended strokes about 1/150 of frame width, varied in length, following the form, never stripes. Dark contour thicker on the shadow side, 1/800–1/300 of frame width. Warm key from upper left. Palette: plaster #E2C492, oak #764626, slate #28322C, brass #E2B046, ink #341E18; oak and ink darks about a third of the frame; sky plaster cooled with slate, water light slate, skin plaster plus brass. Grainy board (±3%), warm vignette. Default one-point perspective, period detail, haze, secondary figures if the story has any. Each figure is drawn once and moves whole (breathing, sway, sliding along a path). Camera: one long, smooth push in. References: J. C. Leyendecker, Norman Rockwell. Avoid: stiff cut-out figures, even outlines, muddy colour.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. push | 2.5 seconds

One continuous push from the whole betting parlour (odds board, betting cages, Gondorff in his window) in to Clawd as Hooker, who lays a finger beside its nose, then tips its hat to the camera.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Johnny Hooker: pale fedora, red bow tie, braces. There is no cut, only one push. As the camera arrives it gives the con men's signal, a finger beside the nose, then tips its hat and blinks at the lens, letting the audience in on the con.
