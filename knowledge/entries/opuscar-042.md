# 042 | Midnight Cowboy: Warhol Photo-Silkscreen, Double Elvis

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Street action becomes a repeated photographic screenprint image, making the composition itself undergo a change of meaning.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Photographic halftones, misregistered brushed colors, a silver-painted ground, and uneven squeegee marks establish the medium. Repetition grows from two figures to three; the source explicitly uses it as composition.

The record separates an uneven photographic ink layer from hand-painted color beneath it, then repeats the subject as composition. A model can explore either the surface relationship or the repeated-figure arrangement, or combine them for a reason in the current task. The source makes their different contributions available for that choice.

A cab's sudden stop and a reaction first establish an event. The setting then fades, the figure takes a gunfighter pose, and pictorial repetition begins. The complete case helps distinguish several people in a scene, deliberate repetition of one image, and an unintended identity error in video.

### Ingredients

- Off-register colour blocks

### Original author's design explanation

Warhol's photo-silkscreen: a black halftone photo printed unevenly over loose flat colour blocks on silver, the image repeated two and three times. The film has a Factory-style party in it, and his Elvis is a cowboy.

### Complete original style prompt

Andy Warhol photo-silkscreen. Two plates: a high-contrast black halftone photograph at 45°, dots about 1/200 of frame width, few mid-tones; and hand-painted flat colour blocks underneath, offset about 1/100 of frame width with wobbly edges: taxi yellow #F3BE14, skin pink #F2A197, cream #EFE6CF, red #E2462B, on brushed silver paint #BABCC0 with a soft sheen. The one hero subject takes the strongest block colour. Uneven squeegee pressure: dots flood into blotches in places, drag into dry horizontal streaks and pinholes elsewhere, over faint canvas weave. Repetition is the composition: the same figure or object, full-length, printed side by side two then three times, slightly overlapping, on empty silver. Settings appear only as drained-to-silver prints in the repeat. Camera: handheld shake, a push in, flat frontal tableaux. Reference: Andy Warhol, *Double Elvis* (1963). Avoid: clean even halftone, perfect registration, uniform crispness.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. street | 1.1 seconds

Sixth Avenue crosswalk: a yellow cab brakes in, Clawd (Joe Buck) jumps back and stares as Ratso slaps the hood; handheld shake, slow push.

#### 2. taxi | 0.6 seconds

Closer on Ratso yelling into the cab, cigarette on his lip, finger pointing ("I'm walkin' here!"); push in.

#### 3. warhol | 0.8 seconds

The street drains to silver and prints itself into Warhol's Double: two overlapping Clawds in Stetsons with revolvers, then three; flat frontal frame.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Joe Buck: black Stetson, red neckerchief and suede fringe on its arms. First the film's "I'm walkin' here!": cab, jump back, slap, yell. Then the image turns into a Warhol print, with Clawd in Elvis's gunslinger stance, printed twice, then three times.
