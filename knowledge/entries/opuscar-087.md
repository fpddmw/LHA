# 087 | Birdman: 3D Backstage Long Take

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Backstage lights, doorways, and camera motion turn space into performance; the source retains both a long-take intention and a particular shot arrangement.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Narrow passages, old walls, pipes, and alternating warm and cool practicals reference continuous movement. Retreating, turning toward a drummer, letting a figure pass, and following place the viewer within the character's route.

The general prompt proposes uninterrupted long takes, while the specific shots include changes to a back door and a square. Both accounts remain available. Their difference supports study of intent and segment implementation.

### Original author's design explanation

A detailed 3D backstage of a Broadway theatre shot as a handheld long take, warm tungsten against cold fluorescent, ending in the glare of Times Square. Birdman is remembered for looking like one unbroken shot, so the style is the camera move itself.

### Complete original style prompt

Real-time 3D, one unbroken handheld long take, after cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki (*Birdman*, 2014). A narrow, lived-in passage (default a backstage corridor; an outdoor subject gets its nearest interior, e.g. a stairwell): chipped blood-red enamel dado #6e2a22 on the lower third, stained nicotine plaster #cdb98e above, pipes, cable trays, doors, pinned notices. Light only from practicals every 2–3 m: warm caged tungsten bulbs, one cold flickering fluorescent tube, a red exit sign #ff2a1a; the far door glows. Mid-key: plaster light near lamps, never below mid-grey between them; floor visible. Wide lens (18–24 mm), slow sway (±1°), soft ambient occlusion, light bloom, fine grain (±3%). Over 20–40 s, in any order: lead the subject walking backwards, whip aside to a doorway, let it brush past the lens, turn and follow. Avoid: a locked-off camera, blank walls, repeated textures, a crushed dark frame.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. oner | 1.3 seconds

Handheld medium shot backing down the corridor ahead of Clawd in its Birdman helmet, whipping left to the jazz drummer in his alcove, then swinging back as Clawd passes the kit and brushes past the lens.

#### 2. door | 0.7 seconds

Cut to behind Clawd at the stage door under the red EXIT light; the camera drifts sideways as Clawd shoves the door open onto white glare.

#### 3. times | 0.5 seconds

Cut to Times Square at night: wide on Clawd on the sidewalk with walls of LED screens behind it; it takes a step and looks up at the screens while the handheld camera drifts slightly.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Riggan in a slate-blue Birdman helmet with a gold beak and feather wings. The corridor runs as one fast handheld move with the drummer's swing in frame; the shove on the stage door is the beat that cuts us out into Times Square, where Clawd stops and looks up.
