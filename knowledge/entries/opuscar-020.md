# 020 | Gentleman's Agreement: 1940s Graphite Magazine Illustration

Magazine pencil illustration connects the drawing process to character reactions, with conflict developing through expressions and glances.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Construction lines, successive hatching, rubbing, and eraser lifts progressively establish a lobby. The key rack, reception desk, and onlookers help explain the ensuing scene.

The source makes pencil darkness through successive hatching and rubbed tone, with pressure changing the contour. This can inform a new drawing through the direction and density of marks on forms. The opening construction animation and colored protagonist are independent decisions; a finished graphite treatment can be developed from the same material relationships.

Two reaction shots convey a polite refusal and an onlooker's sideways glance; the protagonist moves little. Meaning can depend on responses between people. Reading the description together with the shots makes this relationship clear.

### Original author's design explanation

Graphite pencil in the manner of 1940s magazine illustration: construction lines, near-parallel hatching passes, rubbed darks and eraser lifts on toothy paper. The drawing builds itself around a polite refusal at a hotel desk; Clawd alone is in coloured pencil.

### Complete original style prompt

Graphite pencil like a 1940s magazine illustration, finished and controlled. The drawing builds in the first 1-1.5 s, then holds: faint perspective rays and head ovals, pressure-tapered contours (about 1/600 of frame width) overshooting at corners, then 1-4 near-parallel hatching passes (more for darker), about 1/300 of frame width apart, following the form, then rubbed tone and eraser lifts. The finished frame is toned nearly edge to edge: setting fully drawn in mid-greys, about 15% dark accents, only the outer tenth fading to loose margins. Paper #F2EEE5 with a light tooth; lead #2C2B2E. Only the hero the story centres on wears coloured pencil (e.g. #D97757) on its clothing; skin stays graphite. Faces carry the drama. Camera: a slow ease out, then cuts to close-ups. References: illustrators Al Parker and Robert Fawcett. Avoid: cross-hatch grids, scribbly sketching, a near-empty page.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 1.1 seconds

Wide on the hotel lobby being drawn as we watch, from construction lines to hatching: Clawd, in a PRESS-card fedora, holds its notepad up to the front desk; the camera eases back.

#### 2. clerk | 0.8 seconds

Push in from the manager to his close-up: the slight bow, the smile, the eyes that don't smile, every key still on the rack behind him.

#### 3. lady | 0.7 seconds

Locked-off medium on the lady in the fox stole by the lift, her eyes turned back toward Clawd.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Phil Green: a fedora with a PRESS card, a spiral pad and a pencil, standing still at the desk. The first shot is the drawing itself appearing around it; then two hard cuts to the witnesses carry the meaning, the manager's polite refusal and the lady's sideways look. Little moves; the beats land on the cuts. Runs 1.8× fast.
