# 084 | The Artist: Polished Silent-Film Black & White

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A modern silent-film tribute inserts intertitles into performance rhythm, combining clean monochrome with an earlier format.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Refined highlights, middle grays, fine grain, and frame boundaries distinguish this treatment from a rough early print. Art Deco intertitles and a studio stage reconstruct a mode of performance and viewing.

A raised hat, title card, dog's response, and standing audience interweave action with writing. Related silent-film references reveal why different degrees of wear, lighting, and rhythm may be chosen.

### Original author's design explanation

Clean, glossy black and white with bright highlights and soft greys, Art Deco intertitles, light flicker and grain; Clawd is the only colour. The Artist was a 2011 love letter to silent Hollywood, so this is a sharper, cleaner silent film than No. 1.

### Complete original style prompt

A modern homage to late-1920s silent film in black and white. Clean tones: glowing highlights, soft mid-greys, deep blacks; a gentle S-curve, grey halation around lights, slight flicker (±3%), fine grain, rare dust, a 1.33 pillarbox inside 16:9. Intertitles on black with an Art Deco border and spaced capitals, in the user's language. Settings: the subject's world staged as grand, ordered interiors or stage sets (e.g. a wrought-iron staircase, a gilt proscenium, a crowd in backlit silhouette); figures in elegant profile. The one hero subject the story centres on is the only coloured thing, wholly in tones of terracotta #D97757; lamps and fire stay grey. Camera: smooth push-ins and pull-backs, title cards flashed in, cuts landing on gestures (a bow, a wave). Reference: cinematographer Guillaume Schiffman (*The Artist*, 2011). Avoid: heavy scratches; sepia tint; colour bleeding into the greys.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. stairs | 1.0 seconds

Wide of the Kinograph staircase, pushing in: Clawd as George on the landing lifts its top hat to Peppy on her way up, extras crossing up and down, Uggie at its side.

#### 2. close | 0.5 seconds

Close on Clawd: the hat comes off in its raised hand, Uggie looking up at it; a slight push in.

#### 3. card | 0.3 seconds

A full-screen intertitle: BANG! in an Art Deco border.

#### 4. dog | 0.5 seconds

On stage, Clawd points a finger-gun and Uggie keels over to play dead; a slow push in.

#### 5. premiere | 0.9 seconds

Wide of the premiere: THE END on the screen, Constance applauding at right, the house rising in silhouette as Clawd bows with its hat raised and its cane, Uggie at its feet; a slight pull back as the curtain starts to close.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays George Valentin: white wing collar, black bow tie, silk top hat, cane, and Uggie the Jack Russell. Five quick cuts at near real speed: the hat lift on the stairs, the BANG! card flashed in, the dog's fall, then the bow as the audience stands. Clawd is the only colour.
