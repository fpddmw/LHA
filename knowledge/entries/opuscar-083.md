# 083 | The King's Speech: Peeling Wall & Gold Broadcast Booth

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Wall surfaces, figure placement, and a changing waveform organize the movement from difficulty speaking to public address.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

A cool gray-green distressed wall and warm golden broadcast room establish different contexts. Off-center placement gives the wall a strong role in the view. A gold line becoming continuous makes vocal change visible.

Consultation room, microphone close-up, and cooperation in the broadcast room form a complete relationship. The case supports visualization of an abstract change while retaining the particular story that gives its wall and waveform meaning.

### Original author's design explanation

Painterly interiors with heavy wall texture: Logue's peeling, many-coloured wall in cool grey-green, then a padded broadcast booth in amber gold with a sprung microphone and fine gold sound waves. It follows Eve Stewart's famous wall and the poster's gold.

### Complete original style prompt

Painterly interiors where the wall carries the image (outdoor subjects move into a related room with a window). Primary: a diamond-tufted padded wall in gold #C9A050 over deep brown #3A2610, brass button highlights, a warm glow behind the sound source (e.g. a hanging microphone). Secondary, for quiet scenes: a cool grey-green wall worn through to blue, pink, ochre and plaster, with damp lines and fine cracks; patches irregular, under a third of the wall. Clean-edged shapes under a subtle brush texture (strokes ~1/120 of frame width, ±5% brightness); faces, small objects crisp. Sound: 4–8 thin gold oscilloscope lines crossing the wall, spiky, later smooth. Figures in a corner, lots of wall. Camera: slow push-ins, one close-up of the source. References: cinematographer Danny Cohen, designer Eve Stewart (*The King's Speech*, 2010). Avoid: centred symmetry; coarse dabbed strokes; map-like peel; neon-bright waves.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. wall | 0.8 seconds

Wide of Logue's consulting room: Clawd hunched at the left end of the old leather sofa with a cigarette, the huge peeling wall above it, a horn gramophone at right; a slow push in.

#### 2. mic | 0.9 seconds

Big close-up of the marble microphone on its sprung ring, Clawd's crown and eyes at left: the gold sound waves come out broken (the stammer), then run smooth; a slow push in.

#### 3. still | 0.9 seconds

Medium wide of the 1939 broadcast room: Clawd with the speech at left, the microphone in the middle, Logue at right beating time with his hands as the ON AIR light comes on; a slight pull back.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays George VI: a small crown, a wing collar and dark tie, a cigarette in the room and the typed speech in the booth. Three hard cuts tell the cure: hunched in a corner, the stammer smoothing out in the waves, then the speech going out with Logue beating time.
