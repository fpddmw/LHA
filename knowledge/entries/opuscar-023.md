# 023 | All About Eve: Hirschfeld One-Line Caricature

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Theater caricature uses continuous-looking lines, figure proportions, and dialogue in open space to organize a party's relationships.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Long lines, black masses, and fine environmental detail construct the image; open paper can coexist with a fully understood setting. One exaggerated trait identifies each person, making posture and proportion central to recognition.

Line weight, filled black shapes, and open paper work together: swelling contours give a figure rhythm, while thin environmental lines describe a busy setting without equally darkening it. A new caricature proposal can specify that distribution and select its own degree of exaggeration. The particular trait exaggerated remains a creative decision about the subject.

The camera passes the guests, finds Eve in a corner, and returns to Margo raising her glass. Placement and viewing relationships participate in the narrative. Written dialogue and appearing lines are this work's temporal choices, whose relation to performance can be studied elsewhere.

### Original author's design explanation

Al Hirschfeld's Broadway caricature: one flowing ink line that swells and tapers, solid blacks against white, lots of empty paper, one exaggerated trait per person. The film is about the theatre, so its party is drawn as a theatre-page cartoon; Clawd is the only colour.

### Complete original style prompt

An Al Hirschfeld-style caricature. Lines are elastic, long and continuous, one stroke per contour: about 1/800 of frame width at the ends, swelling to 1/250 on curves. Dark clothing, hair and big objects go solid black; grey only as sparse parallel hatching. The setting is fully drawn in thin line, architecture, furniture and props in detail, white kept between objects: about 60% paper white, never an empty page. Push one trait per figure (a long neck, a big head). Paper #FBFAF6, ink #131012; only the hero the story centres on has its body or main garment filled flat with one colour (e.g. #D97757) instead of black. Lines write on within the first second, then hold; a short hand-lettered line may sit in the white. Camera: ease back, pan across, push in. Avoid: stiff limbs, stick figures, a bare setting.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. wide | 1.1 seconds

Wide on Margo's party inking itself on, stairs first, then the guests and the piano; 'FASTEN YOUR SEATBELTS, IT'S GOING TO BE A BUMPY NIGHT' is hand-lettered into the white space, with Clawd as Margo on the staircase; slight pull-back.

#### 2. party | 0.8 seconds

Pan right across the guests, Birdie, Max, Addison, Miss Caswell and the pianist, to Eve in the corner.

#### 3. close | 0.7 seconds

Close on Clawd in pearls and black curls on the staircase, raising the martini; slight push-in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Margo Channing: a row of black curls, a string of pearls, a martini, one arm on the banister. The first shot is the drawing being made and the line of dialogue being written; a pan finds Eve watching from the corner, and after the last cut Margo lifts the glass on the beat. Runs 1.8× fast.
