# 063 | Dances with Wolves: Field Journal · Pencil & Watercolour

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Drawing a journal becomes a route into the prairie, where a relationship with a wolf develops through the recorder's viewpoint.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Pencil visible through watercolor, handwritten arrows, and paper traces establish field recording. Color is applied from far to near, making the viewer aware of both the represented scene and its formation as a record.

Visible pencil and transparent color share the work of describing the scene: pressure-varied lines articulate forms, while washes add color without fully covering them. This relationship can support a new illustration with its own subject and palette. The labels, yellowed journal, sunset, and opening color-arrival sequence each add another function and can be considered separately.

The camera approaches as color appears, then enters a full-frame prairie where the figure tips his hat to the wolf. Recording support, image formation, and event remain connected. Cases retaining visible pages or collage frames offer further comparisons.

### Original author's design explanation

A soldier's field journal: pressure-varied pencil, transparent watercolour, yellowed rag paper, inked script and pencil notes in the margins. Dunbar writes and sketches in his diary throughout the film, so the prairie appears as his sketch coming to life.

### Complete original style prompt

A 19th-century field journal come to life: full-colour watercolour over visible pencil. Pencil under-drawing #3A363A, pressure-varied and broken, lightly hatched in shadow, readable through the washes. Brushed washes with soft wet edges, blooms, pooled rims and granulation (not flat polygons) cover about 80% of the frame in rich warm colour. Default magic hour: dusty-rose sky #C88F76 turning lavender above, streaky rose clouds, low sun, ochre-gold land #DEBE87, violet distance, long shadows, opaque pale-gold lifts on sun-facing edges. Yellowed rag paper #F0E4C8, faint stains and foxing, showing through. Iron-gall ink #34221C for small labels and arrows. Signature move, once at the opening (about 2 s): bare pencil, then colour arrives wash by wash, far to near, during a push-in; after that, full colour. References: Bodmer and Catlin field sketches. Avoid: grey, muddy or pale washes; uniform digital outlines; later pencil-only frames.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. page | 1.2 seconds

Push in on the journal's right page: Clawd's self-portrait and the wolf Two Socks start as bare pencil and are coloured in stroke by stroke, sky first, Clawd last.

#### 2. prairie | 1.3 seconds

Cut to the sketch come alive in full colour at sunset: one push from the plain in to Clawd and Two Socks facing each other, the buffalo herd running behind; Clawd tips its hat.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Dunbar: a black cavalry officer's hat with gold cord, the journal in one hand and a pencil in the other. The push and the colouring-in are one gesture; the cut to the living prairie comes mid-phrase, and the hat tip lands on the last beats. 1:1 time.
