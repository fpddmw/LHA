## Editorial reading and design relationships

Moving caustics, teal haze, bubbles, and warm lamps establish an underwater world. Placing the entire subject underwater is a comprehensive adaptation choice; caustics and color can also be understood as individual pieces of knowledge.

Moving caustics are attached to nearby receiving surfaces, while haze and color loss describe distance. These relationships can develop a new underwater appearance with clear spatial roles. Moving an entire scene underwater is a further world-design decision; the source makes both the material effect and that larger adaptation available for consideration.

An embrace, approaching faces, standing before a tank, and hands meeting across glass form a sequence. Light at the contact point connects a material boundary with emotion. The whole account makes these dependencies visible.

### Ingredients

- Real-time 3D (three.js)
- Projected caustics and volumetric light shafts
- Dan Laustsen teal-and-gold grade

### Original author's design explanation

A flooded 1960s bathroom and a lab tank in real-time 3D, soaked in teal with moving caustics, light shafts from above, bubbles and warm practical lamps. Del Toro's film lives in that green-blue water, so the whole segment is shot inside it.

### Complete original style prompt

Real-time 3D, fully under water (land subjects sink into a flooded room or sunken place), in teal: background #041414, hemisphere light #5fb3a6 above and #0c2a24 below; teal fog, reds fading first with distance. A strong source above the surface projects moving caustics: bright cells about 1/20 of frame width on near surfaces, sliding over floor, walls and the subject. Shafts fan down; edges stay dark. Bubbles, motes, the rippled surface from below. Warm gold on 1–3 practicals, at least one large (a lit window or lamp ~1/10 of frame width), plus cyan bioluminescence. Smooth-shaded models, ambient occlusion, shallow focus, modest bloom, grain ±3%. Signature shot: low angle looking up, the subject half the frame height, off-centre beside the light; slow push-ins. Reference: Guillermo del Toro, *The Shape of Water* (2017). Avoid: flat water, caustics as sky stripes, tiny warm accents.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 0.9 seconds

Low-angle wide inside the flooded bathroom: the amphibian man holds Clawd in an embrace under the light shafts, a red shoe sinking; slow push-in.

#### 2. embrace | 0.5 seconds

Cut to a close-up of the faces: his crest and gills against Clawd's corner, the red ribbon bow, bubbles rising; easing in.

#### 3. tank | 0.6 seconds

Cut to the lab: medium shot of Clawd in front of the glowing tank holding a boiled egg, reaching back to lay its hand on the glass; slow push-in.

#### 4. glass | 0.4 seconds

Cut to a close-up of the two hands meeting through the glass as a cyan glow blooms where they touch.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Elisa with a deep-red velvet ribbon and bow, later a boiled egg in hand. Four cuts in 2.5 seconds, a steady 1.25× pace: the embrace under the light, the faces, the tank, then the hands, with the cyan glow on the touch as the closing beat.
