## Editorial reading and design relationships

Large transparent washes precede fast ink drawings of buildings and figures. Displacement, overlap, and paper white make an active surface. The treatment draws on a painting method and on the cited source of the film's ballet sets.

The displaced washes and continuous ink describe the same forms with different boundaries. A model can borrow that visible tension for a lively illustration, or compare it with the more closely structured glazing of 053. Saying which layer moves away from which contour explains the chosen appearance more clearly than simply naming watercolor; the amount and direction of displacement remain task-specific choices.

Dance steps and flicks of the brush connect the square, dancer close-up, and street. Form and action rhythm support each other. Related material uses offset line and color for social realism, offering a comparison of their different expressive roles.

### Ingredients

- Cold-press watercolour paper

### Original author's design explanation

Raoul Dufy's watercolour: fast blue-black ink lines over big transparent patches of cobalt, lemon and rose that never line up with them. The film's closing ballet was designed after Dufy, so the Place de la Concorde and its dancers are painted his way.

### Complete original style prompt

Paint in Raoul Dufy's watercolour manner: colour first, line second, never aligned. Transparent washes (wet edges, pooled rims, granulation) cover most of the frame, 10-40% oversized, slid one way by about 1/30 of frame width: cobalt #2258CE for sky and water, lemon #FCE02E for light and ground, rose #EC3A7C for warm planes. Overlaps mix violet or green, never brown; keep the hero's wash off blue. Then draw the whole scene densely in quick, thin blue-black ink (about 1/700 of frame width) with flicked ends: buildings, windows, figures, extras; straight edges overshoot corners; trees and clouds as looped strings. Ink #1E1E3A; bare paper 20-30%, skin included. The hero the story centres on gets its own wash (e.g. #D97757). Figures bounce. Camera: small pushes, cuts on the beat (or every 2 s). Avoid: muddy colour, lines tracing the colour, sparse line.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 1.0 seconds

Wide on the Place de la Concorde: dancers whirl round the fountain below the obelisk and the Eiffel Tower; Clawd as Jerry, in a cobalt cap with a brush, hops beside Lise in yellow; small push-in.

#### 2. close | 0.8 seconds

Close on Clawd mid-hop, the brush trailing a ribbon of paint, Lise's arm reaching in; slow push-in.

#### 3. street | 0.8 seconds

Cut to a flag-hung Paris street in pink, lemon and blue, Clawd hopping down the middle among strollers; slow push-in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Jerry from the poster: Gene Kelly's cobalt cap tipped back, a rose neckerchief, a painter's brush that swings a ribbon of paint. It hops on every beat through all three shots, so the dance itself is the cutting rhythm: the square, the close-up with Lise, then the bannered street. Runs 1.8× fast.
