# 064 | The Silence of the Lambs: Hand-coloured Natural-history Copperplate

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Natural-history numbering and engraving extend into figures and a cell, turning specimen observation into confrontation.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Fine hatching, stippling, thin coloring, numbered details, and captions establish ordered observation. The moth connects the plate to the cell, carrying the visual language through both settings.

The view moves from moth to figures separated by glass, then continuously approaches Lecter's face. Distance and emotional intensity change together. The case shows a medium's structure continuing across spaces and acquiring a different role.

### Original author's design explanation

Fine engraved hatching and stipple under thin hand-coloured tints, with plate numbers and Latin labels on yellowed paper. The poster's death's-head moth belongs on a specimen plate, and the same cold engraving carries on into Lecter's cell.

### Complete original style prompt

A hand-coloured natural-history copperplate. Every object in engraved warm black-brown line: dense parallel and cross-hatching (spacing about 1/400 of frame width, thicker in darks), stipple for soft tones, hatched shadows beneath; sky bare or sparsely lined. Flat watercolour tints inside the line, medium saturation, clearly coloured: paper #EFE3C4, umber #5A3A22, rust #B4431E, yellow #E6AD24, grey-violet #7D6F8E. Plate: the subject as Fig. 1 in the centre, about 60% of plate height, 3–6 numbered part or object figures around it, each about 1/5 high; spaced small-caps title, numbered legend, bevelled plate mark, light foxing. Even daylight; time of day only shifts the tints' warmth; dark interiors go cold, hard side light. Camera: a still plate where one thing moves (insect, leaf, hand), then a slow push to a key detail. Reference: Moses Harris, *The Aurelian* (1766). Avoid: smooth gradients; garish colour; a tiny vignetted subject.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. plate | 0.9 seconds

The full entomology plate 'Acherontia styx': a live death's-head moth flies off the nightshade and settles on Clawd's head; a slight push-in.

#### 2. cell | 1.6 seconds

Hard cut to the basement: Clawd as Clarice, FBI trainee ID clipped on, stands at the glass; then one continuous push right and in until Lecter's face fills the frame and the thin smile appears.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd is 'Fig. 4' on the plate, then Clarice with an FBI trainee ID. The moth lands within the opening beats; a hard cut to the cell, and the push to Lecter runs as one move with no cut, his smile arriving at the very end. Scene time runs about 1.8× screen time.
