# 014 | How Green Was My Valley: Welsh Valley Gouache

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Welsh-valley gouache connects landscape painting, mining space, and collective walking, and records why the author changed medium.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

The source explicitly says that a planned woodcut was replaced with gouache to avoid repeating another segment. Arrangement across works thus affected a local style choice. Dark mountains, bright fields, row houses, and a descending road organize space; directional opaque strokes maintain gouache's planar character.

An overlook, a pithead close-up, and tracking miners connect regional setting with working life. The case supports both landscape study and consideration of how a collection develops local differences while retaining relationships. The specific palette belongs to this example.

### Original author's design explanation

Opaque gouache in the 1941 poster's gold and green: solid colour masses, coal-black tips, Kyffin Williams' heavy dark hills and broken contours. It replaced a woodcut that repeated No. 3; the scene is the colliers walking home down the valley, singing.

### Complete original style prompt

Opaque gouache after Welsh painter Kyffin Williams. Directional strokes 1/80-1/40 of frame width, 2-5 times as long, ragged, fully covering; broken dark dashes outline buildings, walls and ridges; dark hills flecked with lighter green or slate knife marks. Palette: sky and light pale gold #F1C565 (not mustard), fields green #86A03F, darks coal #1D1B1E, far hills and water slate #48505E, small accents yellow #F6CF2F, other colours mixed from these; the hero the story centres on #D97757, at least 1/8 of frame height. Fields and sky light (~60%), hills and buildings dark. Warm grade under 10%, vignette, grain. Default low late-afternoon side light: fields lit, ridges rim-lit. From high ground in true perspective, a path leading the eye down; small repeated buildings (or walls, rocks) stepping along slopes. Motion: looping smoke or cloud, a lateral track. Avoid: watercolour transparency, vector fills, mud.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 1.0 seconds

From the hilltop among the daffodils: Clawd in a flat cap and red scarf sits in the left foreground holding a daffodil, the street of terraces leading down to the pithead and the coal tip; a slow pull back.

#### 2. pit | 0.5 seconds

Close on the pithead frame and its winding wheel turning, terraces climbing the green slope behind; a locked-off camera.

#### 3. street | 1.0 seconds

Lateral tracking shot down the terrace street: the colliers walk home in three rows, faces black with coal, singing; Clawd walks in the front row as the houses slide past.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays young Huw in a tweed flat cap and knotted red scarf, with a daffodil. It sits above the valley first, then walks down the street in step with the singing colliers. Two hard cuts: valley → winding wheel → street.
