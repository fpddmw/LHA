## Editorial reading and design relationships

Aircraft structure, distant mountains, amber backlight, and birds taking off at different moments make low flight readable. The proposal maintains clear spatial relationships across a broad landscape while specifying aircraft and flock movement.

Two exterior views establish speed and scale. The onboard view then turns to an offered hand and a grasp. Attention moves from environmental experience to a relationship, supported jointly by action and space.

### Original author's design explanation

Real-time 3D flight over a soda lake: a chrome-yellow Gipsy Moth, thousands of flamingos lifting off, hazy amber light. The film's best-remembered sequence is Denys flying Karen over Kenya, down to the hand she reaches back across the cockpits.

### Complete original style prompt

Real-time 3D like 1980s widescreen location photography: true-scale models with fine detail (e.g. struts, wires, rivets); smooth terrain, not low-poly. Default light: late-afternoon sun low behind the hero as seen from camera, pale cream sky, amber haze, distant ridges in 3–4 paler layers, ochre ground with long soft shadows. Pale overall: about 70% of the frame in light mid-tones, shadows warm grey, not deep brown; under a fifth near-white. Grade: filmic curve, low saturation, light grain, soft vignette. Thousands of instanced birds or animals (e.g. pink #F3B3AE with crimson #DF3F55) lift or scatter in staggered waves as the hero passes. Signature shot: over the moving hero's shoulder across open land; then a high chase 10–20 m up and one shot into the sun. Reference: cinematographer David Watkin (*Out of Africa*, 1985). Avoid: cold clean CG daylight; dark sepia; lockstep flocks.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. flamingos | 0.8 seconds

Low chase just above the lake: the yellow biplane skims overhead with Clawd small in the front cockpit, leaning out, as flamingos lift off the water across the lens.

#### 2. backlit | 0.7 seconds

Hard cut to a wide shot straight into the sun: the plane comes head-on over the lake between two rising pink flocks.

#### 3. cockpit | 1.0 seconds

On board, from the rear seat: Clawd in a leather flying cap, brass goggles and a streaming white scarf turns round and reaches back, and Denys's gloved hand takes it.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd sits in Karen's front seat: leather cap, brass goggles, white silk scarf. Two exterior hard cuts sell speed and scale over the flamingo lake; the cockpit shot then holds for the film's gesture, Clawd turning to reach back for Denys on the last beats. The flight clock runs 1.5–1.6× screen time.
