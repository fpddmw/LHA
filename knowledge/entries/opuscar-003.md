# 003 | All Quiet on the Western Front: Kollwitz Anti-war Woodcut

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

An anti-war woodcut combines black-and-white cuts, figure scale, and quiet action to organize a view of grief.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

The source connects its anti-war subject to Kollwitz's prints. Solid black areas, white contour cuts, enlarged hands, and figures cropped by the frame establish an urgent viewing distance. Paper, wood grain, and missing ink retain the physical character of a print. Its forms differ from monochrome designs built with gray mist and smooth gradients.

For a new woodcut proposal, the important choice is how dark masses and light cuts divide the subject. Cuts can separate a hand from clothing or expose a lit edge, while large black areas join forms into a heavier composition. That relationship can be adapted to a new subject without carrying over the original war scene, orange protagonist, or exact black-area percentage.

Faces in a trench, a hand reaching toward a butterfly, and the final cemetery connect events with imagery. Cuts, composition, and action speed need to be read together. The author's choices about gore and action intensity serve this anti-war interpretation; they do not define woodcut subjects in general.

### Original author's design explanation

A woodcut after Käthe Kollwitz: big masses of black, white knife-cut contours, rough grain, with Clawd printed from its own orange block. Kollwitz's German anti-war prints of 1921–22 come from the same war and the same grief as the film.

### Complete original style prompt

Expressionist woodcut after Käthe Kollwitz, *Krieg* (1921-22). 60-70% solid black; white contour cuts 1/300-1/150 of frame width separate forms; faces are white shapes with a few black features; hands large. Figures fill the frame, cropped by its edges. Each cut tapers in and out with chipped edges, is wider the nearer it sits to the light, in slightly uneven bundles. Ink #17130F on paper #E8DFCA; texture is only wood-grain streaks and under-inked speckle (±5%). One colour block (e.g. #D97757) only on the hero the story centres on, flat on its body or clothes, cuts over it; black stays 60%+. Light enters through one opening (window, cloud gap); lamps get a short ring of cuts. The ground under figures stays uncut black. Quiet motion (breathing, a small gesture); slow pans and push-ins. Avoid: grey fog or midtones, even striped hatching, gore.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. faces | 0.7 seconds

Pan along the trench past carved faces: one soldier with his face in his hands, one staring in fear, one asleep.

#### 2. poster | 0.4 seconds

Tight on a soldier's face under the steel helmet, staring straight out (a nod to the 1930 poster); a slow push in.

#### 3. clawd | 0.9 seconds

Medium: Clawd in a Stahlhelm on the firing step slowly reaches toward the loophole, where a white butterfly sits on a tin; a push in.

#### 4. butterfly | 0.7 seconds

Close on the white butterfly at the loophole, Clawd's orange arm entering from the left; a push in.

#### 5. field | 0.6 seconds

Wide over no-man's land to a cemetery of white crosses under a flare; a ghost column fades in, walking away; a slow pull back.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Paul in a German Stahlhelm. The film is held slow (1.2×) and quiet: the reach starts around beat 3 and takes three beats to stretch out, and the ghost soldiers appear only in the last shot. Four hard cuts go from the faces in the trench to Paul's arm, the butterfly and the field of crosses.
