# 036 | Tom Jones: Hand-Coloured Georgian Satirical Print

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Satirical engraving combines line work, hand washes, dialogue, and performance within a shared comic tone.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Undulating incised lines and form-following hatching establish volume. Full washes extending slightly beyond lines retain the relationship between printing and later hand coloring. Exaggerated figures, onlookers, and speech bubbles connect the medium to satire.

Engraved contours and form-following hatching carry structure beneath the colored washes. Slight color spill and the plate-marked paper add a printed-object character. A new design can develop that image construction while separately choosing whether caricature, comic props, or speech balloons serve its subject.

The camera slides along a table toward a respondent, returns to an exaggerated reaction, and exits when a hat covers the lens. Action, writing, and image boundaries all participate in the performance.

### Ingredients

- Hogarth copperplate engraving
- Rowlandson/Gillray hand colouring
- Satirical speech balloon

### Original author's design explanation

A Georgian satirical print: Hogarth's swelling burin lines and directional hatching under bright Rowlandson-style hand colour brushed slightly off the line, plus a speech balloon. The film itself imitates Hogarth's 18th-century England.

### Complete original style prompt

Georgian satirical print: a copperplate engraving, hand-coloured. Brown-black line only: swelling burin outlines about 1/400 of frame width, heavier on the shadow side; hatching follows each form and covers every surface (spacing about 1/250 of frame width), cross-hatched in the darks, ruled on flat areas. Colour covers nearly the whole picture, clear but strong: washes spilling a few pixels off the line in claret #B3262E, brass #D8A93A, flesh #F0C4A4, slate blue #6D8AA6, warm ochre and umber on large surfaces. Sepia laid paper, foxing, plate mark, engraved border. Stage it like William Hogarth: big-nosed caricatures, bystanders or comic props and animals around the subject; one slanted handwritten speech balloon in the user's language. Camera: hold the full plate 2 s, whip in, glide to another detail, close-up. Avoid: even mechanical hatching, blank paper, colour exactly inside lines, muddy colour.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. two | 1.3 seconds

Whip in from the left onto the two-shot across the laden table, Clawd (Tom) tearing into a drumstick; the camera glides across the table onto Mrs Waters as her speech balloon appears ('Another Leg, Mr Jones?') and she winks.

#### 2. clawd | 1.2 seconds

Close on Clawd: heart eyes pop, a swoon and a lovesick nibble, then it doffs the tricorn and claps it over the lens to black; slow push in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Tom Jones: a gold-trimmed tricorn, a lipstick kiss on its cheek, a drumstick in hand. One glide replaces shot/reverse, carrying the two-shot across the feast onto Mrs Waters' wink and balloon. Then the close: heart eyes, a swoon, and the hat comes at the lens, so the next film opens from black.
