# 047 | The Godfather Part II: Hand-Tinted Sepia Photograph

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A hand-colored photograph connects an immigrant crowd, a subjective view, and a name tag, expressing arrival through small gestures.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

A sepia silver-image layer establishes form while loose transparent coloring supplies emphasis. Light aging retains the historical support. Figure proportions, soft volume, and documentary framing distinguish the treatment from cartoon flat color.

Crowd, Statue of Liberty, and the boy's reaction establish looking and response. Looking down at a name tag and up again connects identity to a new setting. The coloring method can be studied separately while the complete case retains these relationships.

### Ingredients

- raymarched 3D figures

### Original author's design explanation

A turn-of-the-century toned silver print with dyes brushed on by hand: sepia, soft lens, silver grain, foxing, a cracked corner, faint red, blue and verdigris. It shows young Vito arriving at Ellis Island in 1901, as in Lewis Hine's photographs.

### Complete original style prompt

A hand-coloured toned silver print from about 1900. Build lit 3D forms, soft key from upper left, then 'print' them: soft focus, light halation, grain heaviest in mid-tones (±5%), full sepia range #281A12, #987048, #EEE0C2; at least a fifth of the frame reaches the dark end, in any light. Figures: real proportions (head 1/7 of body), soft shading. Transparent dyes brushed loosely past edges: the one hero subject strongly (e.g. orange-red #D97757 at 60–80% opacity), a faint touch on one or two props; dyes die in shadow. Light ageing: vignette, silvered edges (grey-blue sheen in border darks), small foxing spots, fine scratches, at most one cracked corner. Frontal, documentary: figures stand still facing the lens. Camera: slow pan across faces. References: Lewis Hine; Augustus Sherman (Ellis Island). Avoid: toy figures, digital fill colour, low-contrast fog, big mould blotches.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. crowd | 0.8 seconds

A horizontal sweep across the upturned faces of immigrants on deck, rising and falling with the ship's roll.

#### 2. liberty | 0.7 seconds

Clawd's point of view: the Statue of Liberty emerging from the harbour fog, tinted verdigris; a slow push.

#### 3. close | 0.9 seconds

Close on Clawd as young Vito in a tweed flat cap, the 'VITO ANDOLINI' tag on its chest; it looks down at the tag and up again, rising on its toes; a slight pull back.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays the boy Vito Andolini: a grey tweed flat cap and a paper name tag round its neck, small among adults. Screen time averages 1.3×, with Liberty at 1×. Hard cuts run faces, statue (point of view), reaction; the last beat is a glance down at its new name, then up at the new country.
