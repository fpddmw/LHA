# 052 | Kramer vs. Kramer: Child's Wax-Crayon Drawing

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

A child's crayon drawing supplies a viewpoint on family life, with the same pan connecting stages of a changing relationship.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Wax catching paper tooth, irregular fill directions, flattened perspective, labels, and arrows construct the child's expression selected by the source. These choices are tied to a child's view of life with his father.

A matching cut connects burnt toast and golden toast through the same object. Cooperation and response explain the change. The case supports study of how drawing implies a viewer and how object relationships sustain a time ellipsis.

### Original author's design explanation

A kitchen drawn by a child in wax crayon: wobbly lines, colour pressed into the paper grain and over the edges, labels and arrows, a smiling sun. It is Billy's view of making French toast with his father.

### Complete original style prompt

A careful 6–8-year-old's wax-crayon drawing on white paper. Crayon catches the paper grain's peaks; light pressure leaves 40–60% paper showing, heavy pressure fills it; overlaps show through. Outlines in black or dark-brown crayon, waxy, wobbling, about 1/300 of frame width. Fills are back-and-forth scribbles, strokes about 1/40 of frame width long and 1/250 wide, rows overshooting the outlines, each area in its own direction, spacing and angle uneven, never a regular mesh. Primary box: red #E0382C, orange #F28A1E, yellow #F7D23A, green #3AA345, blue #2E6BD8. Child conventions: a ground line, flattened perspective, arrowed labels, a smiling sun, one mirrored letter (in Chinese, a reversed stroke), patterns coloured cell by cell. Text in the user's language, in wobbly crayon strokes, not a typeface. Camera: shaky close-ups, then a calm drift. Avoid: neat adult rendering, random scrawl, uniform stipple or mesh, smooth gradients.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. toast | 0.7 seconds

Extreme close-up of the pan the first time: burnt toast, black crayon smoke, eggshells; a shaky handheld slowly pulling back.

#### 2. toast2 | 0.4 seconds

Match cut to the same pan at the end of the film, clean now: a golden slice flips up out of it.

#### 3. still | 1.4 seconds

Father and son side by side under the title 'ME AND DADDY MAKE FRENCH TOAST': Clawd catches the toast on its spatula, Billy beams and a heart pops up, Clawd hops with pride; the camera drifts up to the crayon skyline in the window.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Ted Kramer with a blue apron tie and a spatula; Billy stands on a chair whisking. The first attempt and the last are joined by a match cut on the same pan, so the whole relationship fits in one jump. About 1.35× overall; the catch lands on the cut to the wide shot, then a hop and a glance at camera.
