# 082 | The Hurt Locker: EOD Technical Manual Line Art

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Technical-manual diagrams and successive close-ups organize a high-pressure action through an increasingly close view.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Consistent lines, hatching, limited color, numbers, and detail diagrams establish technical-document reading. Site and tool details share the system, representing a cinematic context of procedure and pressure.

The shots narrow from a full page to the figure and local action; expression and viewing distance build tension. This is a visual-design case. Its prop details and actions do not supply practical operating instructions or evidence of technical reliability.

### Original author's design explanation

A page from an army bomb-disposal manual: even black lines, hatched shadows, stippled sand, numbered callouts, an exploded IED diagram, a little olive and sand. The Hurt Locker is about procedure under pressure, and a manual page is procedure drawn.

### Complete original style prompt

Military technical-manual illustration. Warm off-white paper #EFEADB with a faint engineering grid, uniform black ink #1B1A16 (outlines ~1/700 of frame width, hatching thinner), shading only by hatching, ground by stippling, flat tints of olive #6C6D42 and sand #D9C79C on 20–30% of the drawing; lights as ray lines, not glows. Main figure: a one-point-perspective view of the subject's setting filling most of the page; around it exploded views of the subject's key object along a dash-dot axis, circled numbers on leader lines, a parts table, WARNING and CAUTION boxes, typewriter labels (user's language) and FIG numbers, a black chapter card. Animate on twos: parts slide apart, a tool works, a figure walks. Camera: whole page, then cuts to enlarged insets. References: US Army technical manuals; Kathryn Bigelow, *The Hurt Locker* (2008). Avoid: gradient shading; too much colour; decorative fonts; sparse pages.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 0.8 seconds

The whole manual page, FIG 4-6 'The Long Walk': a soldier in a bomb suit walks down the Baghdad street toward camera while Clawd stands over the daisy chain of shells with its cutters; a slow push toward the street.

#### 2. suit | 0.6 seconds

Close on the bomb-suit soldier walking, his frowning face behind the visor; a slow push in.

#### 3. close | 0.5 seconds

Close on Clawd in the EOD helmet with the visor up: its eyes drop to the firing lead and the cutters come up; a slight push in.

#### 4. wire | 0.6 seconds

Insert of the cutter jaws on the twisted firing lead above the shell: squeeze, snap, the lead parts; a push in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd wears an olive EOD helmet with the visor up and holds wire cutters among six half-buried 155mm shells. Four cuts tighten from the page to the snap: the long walk, the face in the visor, Clawd's eyes, the cut. The snap comes just before the film ends.
