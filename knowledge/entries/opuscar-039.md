### Original author's design explanation

An Old Master oil portrait: smooth glazed modelling, fur painted hair by hair, velvet highlights, a gold chain link by link, varnish and craquelure. Holbein painted the real Thomas More in 1527, and this is his composition.

### Complete original style prompt

Oil portrait after Hans Holbein the Younger. Paint in layers: a toned underlayer, directional strokes following light and shadow, soft glazes, fine detail last; smooth finish, brushwork faint in background and fabric. Render each material: fur hair by hair, velvet folds with sharp light ridges, metal with two highlights per piece, wool and leather with soft nap. Any subject becomes a formal half-length sitter in its own clothes; its setting shows through a window or in an object it holds. Palette: deep green curtain #1F4A26, black #141113, velvet red #6E0C15, gold #B0801E, fur brown #3E2818; warm, fully modelled skin. Soft light from upper left. Signature shot: the portrait against the curtain; then material close-ups, a slow pull back into a dark room lit by warm pools. Warm varnish, light craquelure. Avoid: flat fills, hard digital edges, even light, unmodelled faces.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. close | 0.9 seconds

Extreme close on the face and the SS collar with its Tudor rose pendant: Clawd (More) looks aside, turns its eyes to the lens and blinks; slow push in.

#### 2. pull | 1.6 seconds

The Holbein portrait composition (Tudor cap, fur collar, red velvet sleeves, green damask, letter in hand): a grave nod, eyes drop to the letter, then a slow pull-back reveals the candlelit Tudor room; the candle gutters and the frame goes dark.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Thomas More: a Tudor cap, black gown with fur collar, red velvet sleeves, the SS chain and a sealed letter. After the spinning meadow, stillness: one cut from close-up to portrait, then a single long pull-back, and the guttering candle carries the dark into the next film's night.
