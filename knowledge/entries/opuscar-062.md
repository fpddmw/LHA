# 062 | Driving Miss Daisy: Rockwell-style Oil Illustration

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Views inside and outside an oil-illustrated car narrow to its rear-view mirror, ending on a small change of expression.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

The source combines a 3D vehicle with visible oil marks. Warm light, cool shadows, and a lived-in street establish the setting. Figures and hands retain volume without heavy outlines, and expressions remain legible.

Exterior, back-seat view, and mirror close-up progressively approach the relationship. Relative positions in the reflection and a final smile carry its change. Car and mirror thus serve narrative functions as well as material ones.

### Original author's design explanation

Solid, warm Rockwell realism: clean edges, visible oil strokes, afternoon light through oaks, every neighbour busy with something. The film is two people in one car on a 1950s Atlanta street, which is a Saturday Evening Post cover already.

### Complete original style prompt

Norman Rockwell-style mid-century magazine-cover oil illustration. Near-realistic figures: head about 1/7 of body height, faces and hands modelled from 3–4 value planes, not outlines; readable expressions. Smooth modelling; faint strokes about 1/150 of frame width, following form, each within ±5% of the colour beneath. Default light: warm afternoon sun from the upper left, clear cast and dappled shadows in cool blue-grey; other light keeps a warm key and cool shadows. Palette: cream sky #E6DCC2, brick red #9A4A38, leaf greens, bottle green #1F3A24, soft skin. Gloss reflects surroundings. Background storytelling: busy bystanders where there are people, telling objects when the hero is alone. Light canvas texture, warm varnish, soft vignette. Camera: deep three-quarter wide, slow push, closer cuts ending on a hand or face. Reference: Rockwell's *Saturday Evening Post* covers (1940s–50s). Avoid: cartoon faces; texture filters; flat digital shading; empty backgrounds.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 0.9 seconds

Three-quarter front view of the dark-green 1949 Hudson rolling down an oak-shaded street of brick and white-porch houses; Clawd at the wheel in a chauffeur's cap, Miss Daisy stern in the back window; a slow push-in.

#### 2. inside | 0.8 seconds

Hard cut to the back seat, locked off: Miss Daisy's profile large in the foreground, Clawd driving seen from behind, the street flowing past the windows.

#### 3. close | 0.8 seconds

Hard cut to the rear-view mirror, the poster's framing: Clawd's eyes under the cap brim on the left, Miss Daisy on the right easing into a small smile; a slight push.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Hoke in a navy chauffeur's cap. Three hard cuts move from outside the car to inside to the mirror, tighter each time, and the payoff is saved for the end: Miss Daisy's frown easing into a smile in the mirror on the last beats. Scene time runs about 1.8× screen time, yet the drive stays unhurried.
