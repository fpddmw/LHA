# 033 | The Apartment: Saul Bass Line Titles in Black & White

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Title lines generate office space, which an overhead view then reads as a pattern; a figure and key become the focus.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Incoming white lines form a one-point grid, gray planes, and ceiling lights, turning graphic animation directly into scene structure. Repeated desks establish scale and order; warm color on the protagonist and key directs attention.

After a rapid approach to the figure, an overhead view changes scale and geometric understanding, preparing a transition to the next segment's street grid. The transferable relationship connects graphics, space, and narrative focus.

### Original author's design explanation

Saul Bass's sliding white lines on black build the endless one-point-perspective office of the film's best-known shot, then fill with grey tones. Bass designed this film's titles; the only warm colour is Clawd and its key tag.

### Complete original style prompt

Saul Bass title lines turned into a set. A shot opens on black: thin white lines (about 1/800 of frame width) slide in and grow into a grid; by 2–3 s grey planes fill in and lights switch on row by row. Then hold the finished frame 3 s: mid-to-light grey, flat fills in 4–5 even steps from #140F0D to #F4F1E8, every shape outlined in dark line about 1/600 of frame width. Strict one-point perspective, centred vanishing point; identical elements repeat into depth (desks and people, or posts, lamps, windows). Figures: flat two-tone shapes. The only warm colour is orange-red #E2472A, for the one hero subject and one prop; all light stays white. Camera: a fast push to the hero, a hard cut to top-down, the grid becoming pattern. Avoid: sketchy lines, gradients, colour leaks, skewed perspective, unfilled wireframe.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. push | 1.9 seconds

One continuous shot: white lines build the endless office out of black, grey tones and ceiling lights come up row by row; Clawd (Baxter) on its front-row desk lifts the apartment key on beat 2 and the camera crash-pushes in.

#### 2. high | 0.6 seconds

Hard cut to a straight-down top view: the desks become a grid, Clawd lying face-up on its desk with the key and tag; slow push in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd is C.C. Baxter: a bowler hat and the apartment key with its orange-red tag (51 W 67), sitting on the front edge of its desk. The office draws itself line by line, the key goes up on beat 2 with a crash push, and the cut to the overhead desk grid sets up the next film, which opens on Manhattan's street grid from above.
