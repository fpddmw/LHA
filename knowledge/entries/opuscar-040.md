# 040 | In the Heat of the Night: Three-Ink Risograph Night

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Three-color printing represents light as unprinted paper, while crossing flashlight beams and reactions create confrontation.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

A red field, blue silhouettes, pink edges, and local paper white establish clear plate relationships. Solid silhouettes and overprinted deepest darks are distinguished, translating night illumination into printing logic.

Reserved paper acts as emitted light while dark silhouettes and colored rims define people. The print method and night illumination thus support each other. A new proposal can choose how light cuts through its limited inks; copying the three ink names alone would omit the relationship that makes the scene luminous.

Beams first find the figure, a reverse shot shows the sheriff's reaction, and the view returns to the figure surrounded by several lights. A posture change closes the emotional movement. Light operates as both illumination and an action between people.

### Original author's design explanation

A three-ink Risograph print in deep blue, poster orange-red and fluorescent pink, with halftone, grain and misregistration; light is printed as less ink. It echoes the 1967 poster's orange heat field and black silhouettes.

### Complete original style prompt

Three-ink Risograph: orange-red #EC5032 as the dominant field (about 60%, with drip streaks), deep blue #162C76 for silhouettes and shadows, fluorescent pink #FF48B0 only for rims and halos, on warm paper #F7F2E7. Each ink is halftoned at its own angle, dots 1/300 of frame width, plates misregistered 2–4 px; grain, pinholes, slight boil. Knock orange out under blue silhouettes so they print pure blue; only the darkest accents overprint to near-black. Light is the absence of ink: beams, lamps and lit ground show bare paper, ringed in pink. Figures: blue silhouettes, pink rims; skin is 30–60% orange with a blue shadow step. At cooler times keep the red field; blue rises only into the top of the sky. Camera: handheld behind someone carrying a light, a reverse close-up, a push. Avoid: blue-dominated frames, smooth gradients, a fourth ink, clean registration.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. pov | 0.8 seconds

Handheld flashlight POV out of the dark: a deputy's beam sweeps the depot platform and finds Clawd (Tibbs) by its suitcase; it looks up, eyes wide, and the other beams snap on.

#### 2. sheriff | 0.5 seconds

Reverse close on Chief Gillespie: peaked cap, aviator shades, chewing gum, then stopping; slow push in.

#### 3. standoff | 1.2 seconds

The stand-off, medium wide: Clawd between the deputies and the chief in crossing flashlight beams, the blue cruiser and the Sparta water tower behind; it straightens up and squares its shoulders as the camera pushes in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Virgil Tibbs: white collar points, a narrow dark tie, shirt cuffs, a suitcase at its side. The night carries over from the previous film's snuffed candle. Three cuts build the confrontation: the flashlight finds it, Gillespie stops chewing, and Clawd draws itself up for "They call me MISTER Tibbs!"
