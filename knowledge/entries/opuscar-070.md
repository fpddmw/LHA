## Editorial reading and design relationships

A blueprint elevation gains perspective, color spreads outward from the figures, and ship, light, and fog enter a day-night progression. Diagram and scene remain attached to one spatial object, giving the change of medium structural continuity.

Open bodily poses give way to an iceberg and an alarm within the same shot. Continuous photography contains compressed time and an event reversal. The source's general blueprint proportion and specific example timings retain their respective contexts rather than becoming fixed execution rules.

### Original author's design explanation

White lines on Prussian-blue drafting paper stand up into perspective and develop into a full-colour 3D ship at sunset, then fall into night. The ship is the film's icon, so its yard drawing and the real ship are the same model.

### Complete original style prompt

A blueprint turning into photoreal-leaning 3D. Opening only (1–2 s, under 15% of the film): white edge lines about 1/800 of frame width on Prussian-blue paper (#12306B to #244D8F), hidden lines dashed, figures and terrain as contours, uneven exposure, folds, lettering. A dolly-zoom stands the elevation up into perspective; colour spreads from the hero along a soft warm edge, ghost lines fading within a second. Then the signature look: golden-hour real 3D, low sun, amber highlights #F2B860, warm shadows #3A2530, sun glitter on water, soft mist, shallow focus, gentle bloom, fine grain; then blue hour and night, warm lights coming on. One unbroken move: push in and stand up (blueprint), then, in equal shares, drift low past the hero, rise and orbit, descend. Reference: cinematographer Russell Carpenter (*Titanic*, 1997). Avoid: lingering on the drawing; hard cuts; flat CG light.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. long | 6.7 seconds

One continuous move: it pushes into the bow on the general-arrangement sheet; the drawing unfolds into perspective and develops into sunset from the lovers outward; a low drift on Rose's backlit silhouette with Clawd flying beside her, arms out; it rises and orbits over the port bow as the sun sinks and the ship's lights come on; it crosses the forecastle into night while Rose steps down from the rail; it comes down behind Clawd as the iceberg emerges from the mist, and Clawd turns and raises the alarm.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Jack in a navy wool scarf. Sixteen beats, no cuts: the blueprint has unfolded by 1.5 s and the colour has developed by 2.4 s, with Clawd flying beside Rose; sunset to night is a time-lapse inside the move, and the berg rises in the last two seconds, Clawd's turn and alarm landing on the final beats.
