# 044 | The French Connection: 16mm Chase Under the El

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Camera placement, repeated columns, and spatial motion create the speed of a 3D car chase, while a 16mm appearance adds a documentary tone.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Elevated-rail columns, overhead bands of light, and detailed streets provide continuous references for fast movement. A low forward view, an exterior view back at the driver, and vehicle vibration shift the viewer's position within the motion. Cool-gray grading, residual warm browns, and grain complete the imaging choice.

Intersection, bumper, and character views continue the same chase. Accelerated scene time is the author's code-animation implementation method. The visual objective and the means used to realize it should retain their separate status.

### Original author's design explanation

A full 3D Brooklyn avenue under the elevated railway, graded like grainy 16mm: cold grey, cyan shadows, high contrast, gate weave. The film is remembered for its car chase under the el, and only a real 3D world gives that speed.

### Complete original style prompt

Gritty early-1970s documentary look in a detailed 3D world, finished as pushed 16mm. Grade: low saturation, high contrast; neutral-cold greys #7A7C7A, cyan shadows #1E2A2C, warm-brown mid-tones #6E4A34 in brick, wood, vehicles (at least a quarter of the frame), never an overall blue; the one hero (e.g. #D97757) stays saturated. Default overcast, sky near-white, at any time of day; sun through any overhead grid (track, bridge, pier) falls in hard stripes. Grain clumps about 1/700 of frame width, ±8% brightness; gate weave, flicker, dust, occasional faint ghosts of bright lights. Dense clutter fitting the place. Handheld cameras ride the action: a low 18–25 mm lens rushing forward as repeated objects strobe past, a rig looking back at the subject, a corner wide. References: William Friedkin, *The French Connection* (1971); cinematographer Owen Roizman. Avoid: clean CG shine, blue tint, empty places, mushy grain.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. wide | 0.8 seconds

Wide at the corner: a train crosses overhead as the brown Pontiac skids onto the avenue with its rear tyres smoking; the lens slowly widens.

#### 2. bumper | 0.8 seconds

The famous bumper mount: a low lens rushing up the avenue with motion blur, the el's columns strobing past.

#### 3. window | 0.9 seconds

Medium shot from outside the car through the rolled-down window: Clawd as Doyle in a pork-pie hat, grim at the wheel, the el and storefronts sliding behind, with vehicle shake.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Popeye Doyle: pork-pie hat, angry eyes, both hands on the wheel. The chase clock runs well ahead of screen time, so the speed-up reads as more speed. Three hard cuts, corner skid to bumper rush to the driver, with no pause in between.

### References named by the author

William Friedkin, The French Connection (1971) · Owen Roizman (cinematography) · The French Connection one-sheet (1971)
