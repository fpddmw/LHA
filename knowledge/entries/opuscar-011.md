## Editorial reading and design relationships

The source details light-to-dark plates, knockouts, stepped separations, misregistration, and cotton-paper texture. Flatness comes from printing relationships. Multiple activities in the living room give local areas their own behavior before they converge in a burst of fireworks.

The printing account connects a limited set of inks to planes, shadows, and highlights: light areas reserve a lighter plate, while darker overprints build selected shadows. A new proposal can use that relationship to describe a scene as broad opaque color shapes with visible registration differences. The source's six colors, title band, and plate-arrival animation are further design choices with their own roles.

### Original author's design explanation

A 1930s WPA silkscreen poster: six flat opaque inks, split-fountain gradients, slight misregistration on rag paper. The Federal Art Project posters of 1936–39 were American graphic design of the film's own years, and they keep it apart from No. 10's newsprint.

### Complete original style prompt

A 1936-39 WPA (Federal Art Project) silkscreen poster. Six opaque inks printed light to dark, each later screen cut away around lighter shapes (clouds, sun): cream #F0E2C0 for light, ochre #E0A842 and vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm planes, teal #22686E for water and shade, the hero's own ink (e.g. #D97757, only on the hero the story centres on), brown keyline #322018 last. Shadows: a darker ink overprinted, never grey. Big areas get split-fountain blends: two inks in 2-3 coarse steps, the middle one their overprint. Screens 1-3 px off register; wandering stencil edges, squeegee streaks, warm rag-paper grain (±5%) on every flat. Figures fill 1/2-2/3 of frame height: big planes, crescent shadows, expressive faces. A bold capital title band, user's language. Slow pushes; screens pop in one by one. Avoid: halftone dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 0.8 seconds

The whole Vanderhof living room as a poster: Clawd as Grandpa in round glasses plays harmonica in the teal wing chair, Essie dances on pointe in the window, Kolenkhov shouts the count, De Pinna pops out of the cellar hatch; a slight pull back.

#### 2. harmonica | 0.6 seconds

Close on Clawd with glasses and harmonica, notes drifting up, De Pinna at the edge of frame; a push in.

#### 3. fireworks | 1.1 seconds

Back on the room as the cellar fireworks go off: rockets, starbursts and smoke columns fill it; the camera pulls back.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd is Grandpa Vanderhof: round wire spectacles and a harmonica, crutches leaning on the chair. The whole household keeps doing its own thing at once while the cellar fireworks build through the last shot. Two hard cuts: room → harmonica → fireworks.
