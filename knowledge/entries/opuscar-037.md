# 037 | My Fair Lady: Bob Peak Fashion Illustration

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

Fashion illustration uses fast ink, pools of color, and a lateral figure arrangement to depict a social occasion; hats and binoculars also carry transitions.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Dry ink strokes, black-and-white garment patterns, and a pink watercolor background have distinct roles. Elongation, repetition, and lightly drawn surroundings move attention along a row of poses, connecting the drawing method to clothing design.

A hat brim reveals the scene from black. Glances, a greeting, and simultaneously raised binoculars form the event; a binocular circle then enters the next segment. Clothing functions as image form, social sign, and masking device within the full account.

### Ingredients

- Fast ink line
- Pink and magenta watercolour
- Bob Peak's 1964 poster
- Cecil Beaton's Ascot costumes

### Original author's design explanation

Fast, dry-edged ink line with pink and magenta watercolour washes, splatter and edges fading to white paper. The original 1964 poster was Bob Peak's ink and pink; the scene is Cecil Beaton's black-and-white Ascot.

### Complete original style prompt

1960s fashion illustration after Bob Peak. Fast ink line, thicker in turns, dry-brush tail, a flick off; 1/600–1/250 of frame width, thinning on tight curves. Solid black for dark clothing, hats and stripes; faces bare paper #FBF7F1. Loose, soft-edged watercolour pools, darker at drying edges: pink #EE84B2 as the main field, magenta #CE3076 for hot spots, rose #F4B2CC, a touch of plum #80285C; warm grey for secondary figures and shading; directional splatter. Settings are a few lines in these washes, not literal colour. The frieze is essential: slender profile figures 9–10 heads tall repeated across the frame (the hero plus grey-wash figures or echoes), black-and-white pattern over pink; washes cover 60–80% of the frame. Camera: slow drift along the figures; graphic-mask transitions. Avoid: even-width outlines, flat digital fills, polygon-edged washes, a lone figure on an empty page.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. reveal | 0.8 seconds

Out of black, the Ascot hat brim lifts to reveal Clawd (Eliza) close; it turns its eyes to the lens and dips a small curtsey as the frame settles back slightly.

#### 2. wide | 0.6 seconds

Wide on the Ascot tableau: Clawd in front of two rows of stiff aristocrats in Beaton black and white; slow lateral glide as it lifts its chin.

#### 3. crowd | 1.1 seconds

Pan along the frozen crowd as every pair of binoculars goes up, then the frame closes into two binocular circles.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Eliza: an enormous white hat with striped ribbon, roses and ostrich plumes, a lace collar and hem. The film opens from Tom Jones' black under its own hat brim and closes inside the crowd's binoculars, so The Sound of Music can open within the same two circles. Three unhurried cuts.
