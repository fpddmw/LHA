# 043 | Patton: WWII War-Bond Lithograph

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

The graphic scale of a war-bond poster relates a speaker to an enormous flag; a final pullback reveals the performance venue.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Hard-edged value steps, halftone shadows, paper, and a huge background graphic work together. Saluting and addressing the audience before the flag make proportion central to the design.

The opening withdraws from stripes to reveal the figure; the ending passes beyond the flag to show an empty theater. These two changes of viewing scale connect symbol, person, and stage. Military props and gestures belong to this subject.

### Original author's design explanation

Flat-colour stone lithograph in the manner of 1940s US war-bond posters: posterised folds, a dark key line, halftone shadows, yellowed stock. It fits Patton's opening speech, where a tiny general stands before a stage-high flag.

### Complete original style prompt

1940s US war-bond lithograph poster. Flat inks: red #C2342C, navy #213261, olive #5F5C31, khaki #B7975E, on fibrous, foxed cream #E6D5B3 paper. Three values per form (bare paper, flat ink, ink plus halftone), posterised in hard steps. Brown-black key line about 1/500 of frame width, printed last; shadows as coarse 45° halftone bands (dots about 1/150 of frame width) with crayon grain; colour plates offset about 1/600 of frame width. Default: a symmetrical frontal stage, a small subject before a huge graphic shape filling most of the frame (flag, emblem, sunburst), lights as hard pale shapes or bare paper; outdoors too, light falling as a hard pale pool. Lettering optional, in the user's language. Camera: slow push-ins, one crisp pull-back. Reference: US war-bond posters, 1942–45. Avoid: airbrushed gradients, perfect registration, halftone so fine it reads grey.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. reveal | 1.0 seconds

Red and cream stripes fill the frame (a graphic match from the previous film's Warhol grid), then one pull-back finds Clawd walking up from below frame to its mark before the flag.

#### 2. close | 0.9 seconds

Medium close-up, a slow push with a slight shake: Clawd snaps up the salute under the four-star helmet, holds it, drops it, and the riding crop cracks into its palm with a hard nod.

#### 3. wide | 0.5 seconds

Wide: the camera pulls out past the flag to the whole empty theatre, red curtains, gilt proscenium and footlights, Clawd tiny at centre stage.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Patton: four-star helmet, ribbon bars and medals, ivory-gripped revolver, riding crop. It walks to its mark under the stripes; on the cut to the close-up the salute snaps up, holds, drops, and the crop cracks into its palm with a nod, as if the speech begins. Two hard cuts; the final pull-back shrinks it before the flag.
