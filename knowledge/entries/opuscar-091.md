A travel postcard passes from tabletop object into a moving road scene, with words and support establishing its viewing context.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Linen grain, hand separations, chrome starbursts, and large letters containing scenery establish postcard language. The source ties this to road travel; a room key and the Green Book place the card within a specific traveling life.

One approach enters the driving scene, followed by the sole cut to a concert hall. Medium and location changes play different roles. Albums and journals offer comparisons for entering images.

### Original author's design explanation

A "Greetings from the Deep South" linen postcard that comes alive: woven paper texture, airbrushed saturated colour, chrome sparkles. The film is a 1962 road trip through the South, the same roads these cards sold to tourists.

### Complete original style prompt

A 1950s–60s linen postcard, Curt Teich manner. Linen-weave card: ink only on raised threads, a fine crosshatch about 1/300 of frame width, white specks in the hollows. Saturated hand-separated colour, airbrush blends: sky blue #3fa0dc, grass green #4fae57, pink #f08cb0, teal #2fb5b8 for the main object or water, cream card #f6e4b8 for paper and border. The main object large, side-on, about half the card's height. Star glints on shiny surfaces; registration off 1–2 px. Big navy 3D block letters with white outlines across the top third spell the place or title (invent one if needed), a small scene in each letter, a red ribbon banner above. Open on the card on a desk, push in until it fills the frame and comes alive (water, figures move), then track sideways. Avoid: weave-less gradients, greyish colour, photographic perspective.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. into | 1.9 seconds

One continuous move: the postcard on a motel desk beside a Green Book and a room key; the camera pushes into the card until the picture comes alive, the teal Cadillac pulls away and the camera pulls back into a side-on tracking shot of Clawd at the wheel with Dr. Shirley in the back.

#### 2. piano | 0.6 seconds

One cut to the concert hall: wide on Dr. Shirley at the grand piano with his trio under red velvet curtains, Clawd standing at the wing watching; slight push-in.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Tony: a slicked black pompadour, short sky-blue sleeves, a lit cigarette, one hand on the wheel. The push into the postcard carries us into the road without a cut; the single cut lands on the concert hall, where Dr. Shirley plays and Clawd watches from the wing.
