# 054 | Chariots of Fire: 1924 Paris Olympics Stone Lithograph

[Knowledge map](../README.md) · [Reading manual](../READING.md)

An athletic scene gradually becomes an Olympic poster, with print grain, figure construction, and slowed details expressing running.

## Editorial reading and design relationships

Values in this lithographic proposal arise from grain coverage, unlike posters organized primarily by flat plates. Classical proportions, a lateral formation, pale structures, and splashes keep the movement legible.

Faces, feet, close-ups, and a whole team in profile offer different information about running. The final printed lettering and border change the image's identity. Slow motion and the poster reveal need to be understood with the shot sequence.

### Original author's design explanation

A four-colour stone lithograph after Jean Droit's 1924 Paris Olympics poster: classical bodies, a few inks, clumped stone grain, ragged registration on cream paper. Chariots of Fire builds to the 1924 Games, so its beach run becomes that poster coming to life.

### Complete original style prompt

1920s stone lithograph poster in four inks: sea blue #245A8C, ochre #D49640, deep brown #40271B, warm red #E47E62, on aged cream #F1E7D1, misregistered about 1/700 of frame width. Every tone is ink grain, never a flat fill: specks about 1/1000 of frame width in soft clumps, covering 20–40% of pale areas, 50–80% of mid-tones, nearly all of solids (with pinholes); no stray specks on bare paper. Roles: blue for sky, water, cool shadow; ochre for ground and skin; brown for outlines, darks, clothes; red sparingly. Values calm and pale. Classical figures: ochre lit side, brown core-shadow line down each limb, head 1/8 of body; groups in a row. Slow motion carried by spray, dust, hair, cloth. Camera: long-lens front views, ground-level shots; optional serif title band. Reference: Jean Droit, 1924 Paris Olympics poster. Avoid: coarse speckle, pictogram figures, vector fills.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. faces | 0.7 seconds

Long-lens front view: the pack comes toward camera along the surf, Clawd among them with a white headband and race number 98; a slight pull back.

#### 2. feet | 0.6 seconds

Low and tight on bare feet splashing through the shallows, reflections in the wet sand; the camera slides sideways.

#### 3. liddell | 0.5 seconds

Close on Liddell, head thrown back and mouth open, Abrahams behind him; a slight pull back.

#### 4. still | 0.8 seconds

Side view of the whole line with Clawd in the middle; as the camera pulls back, the 'JEUX DE LA VIIIe OLYMPIADE · PARIS 1924' poster band prints in over the frame.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd runs in the pack with a white headband and a pinned number 98. The run plays at 1×, so it reads as the film's slow motion. Four hard cuts (faces, feet, Liddell's head-back stride, the side-on line), and the poster band printing over the last shot turns the scene into the 1924 poster.
