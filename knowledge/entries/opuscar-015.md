## Editorial reading and design relationships

A gray-blue shadow plate, dark-green contour plate, and limited accents give figures, light shafts, and a damaged roof one printing language. The source represents light by locally removing the shadow plate, specifying how illumination works within this medium.

Here a light band is represented by removing the shadow plate from the area it crosses. That joins illumination to the printing method. It can inform a new illustration in which shafts of light reveal lighter paper or ink, while the source's aircraft, frontal figures, and three-plate palette remain specific compositional choices.

Engine noise interrupts singing and prompts a look upward; the next shot enters that view. Action motivates the spatial transition, while the juxtaposition of village life and aircraft gives it meaning. The shot list explains more of the design than its palette alone.

### Original author's design explanation

A WWII British Home Front lithograph poster: flat brick red, deep green, cream and grey-blue, with a grainy crayon shadow plate. It fits the film's last scene, a village singing in its bombed church while Spitfires pass overhead.

### Complete original style prompt

A 1942 British wartime stone-lithograph poster. Simple, strong shapes from three plates, 1-3 px off register: a colour plate with all flat fills; a grey-blue crayon plate darkening every shadow with short slanted stone grain; a green-black key line about 1/500 of frame width around every shape, figures and clouds included. Palette by role: cream #EBDFC2 paper and highlights, grey-blue #8497A6 sky and shadow, brick red #A23F2D and deep green #34503F as accents, at most a quarter of the frame; browns and skin mixed from cream and brick; key #1E2422. Scenes may be full. Airbrushed skies; only the paper grain boils at 12 fps. Pale light bands, angled from the source, cancel the shadow plate. Figures stand frontally. Camera: slow drifts, a tilt up. References: Frank Newbould, Abram Games. Avoid: dot screens, clean vector edges, big saturated fields.

### All shots in their original order

#### 1. still | 0.8 seconds

Wide down the bombed nave: the village sings from the pews under shafts of sun; Clawd sits in the front pew in a lace-trimmed straw hat with hymn book and red rose, singing. The camera eases back slightly.

#### 2. close | 0.6 seconds

Close on Clawd with the rose: the engines come over, it stops singing and looks up toward the roof. Slight push-in.

#### 3. roof | 1.1 seconds

Looking straight up through the torn roof: snapped rafters frame the sky as a V of Spitfires crosses it. Slow push with a slight roll.

### Direction and handoff

Clawd plays Mrs. Miniver: a lace-trimmed straw hat, a hymn book and the red 'Miniver' rose. It sings through the wide shot; on the cut to the close-up it hears the engines and looks up, and the third shot is its view through the hole as the Spitfires pass. Hard cuts, linked by one upward look.
