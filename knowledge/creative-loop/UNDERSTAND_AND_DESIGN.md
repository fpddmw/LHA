# Recast the source into another world or medium

The task is a recast: take an existing video and make it a coherent work in the world or medium the request names. The result should read as that world or medium from its first second to its last, while still being recognizably this moment. It is neither a faithful edit nor a new scene. Both ends are scored: how fully the world was rebuilt, and how stable and clean the video is.

Use this manual before the first generation and whenever the design changes. Keep the working design in notes/workspace in the order below.

## 1. Fix the moment

Write the source's skeleton in a sentence or two: who acts, what happens and in what order, how the space is arranged, where the camera is and how it moves. A viewer who knows the source should find this moment in the result. See [moment](../experience/curated/entries/P-moment.md). Roles, arrangement and key contacts are the skeleton. Poses and gestures are performance material you can use. Old-world functions (a hoop, a keyboard, a laptop) are yours to replace when the new world gives the action another form.

## 2. List the planes and give each a counterpart

List every visible plane: each person, held props, furniture or vehicles, ground, middle distance, background, sky and light. For each, write what it becomes in the target world and how that world builds it. A plane you leave out stays source-like and the result reads as a collage. See [every plane](../experience/curated/entries/P-every-plane.md) and [counterparts](../experience/curated/entries/P-counterparts.md). Use the [meaning guide](../guides/meaning.md) for roles and event logic.

## 3. Write the medium's construction logic

Name what makes a mark, a surface and a shadow in the medium, and what the light does to each plane. Adjectives and style names alone leave the renderer to guess. See [medium logic](../experience/curated/entries/P-medium-logic.md). Use [theme](../guides/theme.md), [media](../guides/media.md), [time](../guides/time.md), [combination](../guides/combination.md) and [design expression](../guides/design-expression.md) for relationships you can adapt. Transfer the relationship, not the source work's setting.

## 4. Keep the people the same people

Preserve people at role level and keep short identity wording. Spend design effort around the face rather than in a long facial inventory. A costume change is part of the task. See [identity](../experience/curated/entries/P-identity.md).

## 5. Decide the checks before you generate

Decide in advance what you will look at in the result: each person, held props and contacts, the first and last second, the planes you did not describe, and every item the request names. See [worst second](../experience/curated/entries/P-worst-second.md).

## Use experience for the starting choices

The [experience map](../experience/curated/README.md) lists common principles, then your renderer's capabilities and traps, then conditions for short and long requests and for the family of world. The [renderer profiles](../experience/curated/renderers.md) give the starting preferences. Entries state their evidence and how far it reaches; some are hypotheses. Check an entry's exceptions against this scene before applying it.

## A short or long request

A short request names the world and leaves the layout, counterparts and planes to you ([short requests](../experience/curated/entries/C-short.md)). A long request lists planes, costume, light and camera behavior; treat the list as the checklist you will be held to ([long requests](../experience/curated/entries/C-long.md)).

Submit the concrete English prompt once the direction is worth trying, then use the actual result to develop the next design.
