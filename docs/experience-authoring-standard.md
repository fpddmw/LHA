# Writing useful experience

Shared by the three project maintenance Skills. Scope: human-initiated maintenance of development experience. This standard supports semantic judgment; it is not a Planner form, automated promotion policy or mandatory sequence.

## What deserves an entry

An entry earns attention by changing a consequential distinction: what makes a scene readable, which condition an edit requires, why two apparently similar failures call for different responses, or why an apparently plausible correction repeats the original problem. A useful explanation connects observed outcomes to a choice the reader could otherwise misjudge.

Start from actual requests, inputs, exact prompts and resulting evidence. Planner's intention is a source of hypotheses; the submitted prompt specifies the actual intervention. Inspect comparisons that could overturn the explanation. Read the necessary original records rather than treating prior summaries as observations. Open videos or frames if making a new visual claim; otherwise attribute the saved judgment and preserve its coverage limits.

Do not manufacture an entry quota. A batch can strengthen an existing account, narrow its scope, expose a bad explanation or add no useful experience. A successful outcome may require as much explanation as a failure. General advice already obvious to a capable model usually belongs nowhere; a stable interface fact belongs in the operating manual.

## From event to understanding

The study trace provides a concrete editorial example:

- Event summary: a robe hid a chair, and a later jacket version showed the chair.
- Useful interpretation: an authored costume may compete with the visual support relations needed to read the activity. More preservation wording does not remove the conflict in the selected realization.
- Overreach: robes always harm preservation, or source restarts are the cure. All study attempts used source input, and multiple descriptions changed with the costume.

Likewise, naming glasses can reconstruct an accessory category without preserving an individual. This distinction changes what evidence to seek in a candidate and what a new description supplies. 'Use fewer words' does not explain the same issue. A shorter alternative needs its own evidence.

Compare omitted instructions with explicit but unrealized instructions; inherited defects with newly introduced ones; changes in realization with a restatement of the same realization. These are useful contrasts, not required labels to fill in. Opus's valuable contribution was this contrastive, cross-case interpretation, not a particular card layout or a claim to its private reasoning process.

## Write for progressive reading

Use a stable ID and revision, a short discovery summary, a readable explanatory account, and linked evidence. The account should make its practical consequence apparent. Let its structure follow the phenomenon. Avoid a compulsory When/Do template that turns a conditional observation into an unconditional order.

Keep the main insight direct. State renderer/setup and consequential limits where they qualify the claim. Avoid repeating caveats in every sentence. Reader-facing summaries must contain the actual distinction; 'apply S1' and 'use caution' are insufficient.

Separate evidence dimensions in ordinary prose:

- Recorded observation: what a saved review, independent judge or actual inspection found, with its identity and coverage.
- Interpretation: the proposed relationship explaining the observations.
- Strategy hypothesis: what could be tried because of that interpretation, with any existing outcome evidence.
- Interface fact: what information or controls the renderer actually receives.

Content approval concerns whether the explanation is suitable to read. Experimental support concerns what its evidence establishes. Neither status substitutes for the other. Do not infer success from a count threshold or silently upgrade old cards labeled 'observed'.

## Evidence and comparison

Preserve source task, request, input ancestry, submitted prompt, candidate, renderer configuration, judgment provenance and relevant counterexamples. Cite exact local records or immutable excerpts. Preserve media hashes and frame times when available; say when files are unavailable or frames have not been newly inspected. Sparse timestamps do not establish aligned motion.

Counts come from deterministic records with explicit denominators. Distinguish sources, tasks, runs and attempts. Unknown is not failure. Several attempts on one source are not independent replications. Separate online reports, independent assessments and human inspection. Different evaluation protocols remain separate cohorts.

An association between reading a card and a better output is not an isolated card effect. Hard cases can cause both more reading and worse outcomes. Explicit rejection can be sensible use. A changed action may already have appeared in the previous plan. Record these alternatives before recommending a new wording or a targeted comparison. New rendering, scoring or controlled comparisons need separate authorization.

## Curation and approval

Prefer one explanation with renderer-specific evidence over several copies of the same rule. Split claims whose conditions or evidence differ. Keep an old-ID mapping and a reason for each merge, narrowing or retirement. Preserve original evidence and historical text; revise the interpretation as an explicitly versioned artifact.

Propose concrete edits with the exact wording, scope and implications for readers. Stage changes until the user approves the content and activation scope. Running tasks use their saved snapshot. An approved addition is a normal reviewed repository/catalog change, not an automatic publication transaction.

Maintenance uses development material authorized for that purpose. Held-out/test outcomes are excluded from experience-writing input. If a user intentionally repurposes them, record the loss of held-out status before doing so. Current-batch final scores do not feed the current Planner. This policy requires no new experiment scheduler.
